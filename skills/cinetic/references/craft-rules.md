# Craft rules: timing, smoothness and pacing

Covers the measured norms of top-tier launch films: how long shots run and how often the picture changes, how moves are eased and handed across cuts, how long words stay and how they arrive and leave, how transitions are hidden, how the camera and the product move, how colour and light change over time, and how frames are laid out. Read it in Step 1 when you shape the beat sheet, in Steps 3–4 when you set durations, curves and staggers, and in review whenever a film feels slow, busy, floaty or cheap.

Every number here was measured frame by frame in top-tier launch films, from 9 s social teasers to 90 s feature films, and converted to one convention:
- **f** is a frame at 60 fps. Speeds are **px/f at 1080p and 60 fps**. Sizes are shares of frame height (**FH**) or frame width (**FW**).
- A value reads "median (range)" across the films.
- **Frame difference** is the mean absolute luma change between consecutive frames on a 320 px-wide greyscale copy, on a 0–255 scale. "Near-still" means a frame difference under 0.5.
- A **Skill check** line marks a measured norm that differs from a default in `SKILL.md` or another reference, and gives the measured range. The skill's default stands until you have a reason to move; this file is that reason when you need one.

The numbers describe what the best films do. They are not a licence for the looks those films sometimes used that the Hard bans exclude (glow, glass, multi-hue gradients, serif and italic type): every rule below is stated in a ban-safe form.

## 1. Pacing and structure

1. **Shot length comes in two tempos.** The median shot is 1.46 s (1.1–2.8 s). Cut-driven films sit at 1.1–1.6 s (66–96 f); continuous-take and morph-chain films sit at 2.3–2.8 s and carry each take with beats inside it. The shortest shot is 0.67 s (0.27–1.27 s). Cut-driven films never hold one framing past 2.2–3.2 s; continuous takes run 5–9 s.
2. **Change the picture about every 0.7 s, whatever the cut count.** Scene changes run 5.2 per 10 s (2.5–7.8); dense multi-cell loops and strobed sign-offs reach 20–22. Hard cuts run 3.3 per 10 s (0–22), and a few of the films have no visible hard cut at all. Even a film whose median shot is 2.75 s lands a new visual beat about every 0.7 s (about 40 beats in 27.5 s); a dense loop changes something every 0.33 s. A one-take film that lets 1–4 s pass with a locked camera and only a caption changing ends up with 57% near-still frames and little rhythmic contrast. Consistent with the skill's 45–60 events per 30 s.
3. **Shots under 0.45 s carry no copy.** Shots of 0.27–0.43 s work as punctuation (a crash zoom, a caret-follow, a strobe frame). With a word on them they barely register: a word fully legible for about 0.13 s before a whip is lost.
4. **The first frame already moves.** First visible change: median 0.10 s (0.02–0.43 s); nearly half change within 2 frames and all by 0.43 s. First readable word: median 0.4 s. The first hard change (a cut, a polarity flip, a scale reveal or a surge) lands at a median 1.3 s (0.1–4.1 s), and by 0.8 s in nearly half. The premise reads by about 2 s (0.3–6 s); films that take longer than 2.5 s hold attention with a physical gag or a polarity flip in the meantime. A 2.7 s still title before any motion reads as a slow hook. Consistent with the skill's hook rules.
5. **The product appears early unless a problem act earns the wait.** Product UI is on screen at a median 2.8 s (0–21 s). Films that hold it back run a 10–19 s problem act first and reveal the product or name at 23–26% of runtime.
6. **Act lengths.** The opening act (hook, problem or thesis, before the product or the turn) runs a median 12% of runtime (5–47%); two-act films switch at 36–58%. The proof or demo runs 35–80%. The outro (final claim, tagline and lockup) runs a median 15% (9–33%), and the end card alone 9% (2–13%). Two acts in different visual worlds read as two films unless one object, shape or colour carries across the switch.
7. **The energy curve has two peaks and a dip.** An early peak in the hook or reveal (10–25% of runtime); a designed lull of 1–4 s at a median 65% (55–85%), usually inside a music breakdown; the last and biggest peak at a median 82% (35–90%); then a calm outro of 8–20%. Every film whose last peak came before 60% sags after it. Inside that arc, each act opens on a burst and decays into a reading plateau (a sawtooth).
8. **Low-energy stretches stay under 4 s.** Lulls of 1–4 s read as breath. Stretches of 5–10 s with only small typing, static rows or a loader read as a sag, and the most common place for one is the middle of the product demo.
9. **Motion energy has a budget.** Median frame difference 1.4 (0.35–2.9), p90 6.0 (2.4–13.3), near-still frames 27% (4–57%). Calm, premium films sit at a median of 0.35–0.5 with over 50% near-still frames, and still spike sharply on transitions (p90 ≥ 2.4). High-energy films sit at a median of 2.2–2.9 with under 15% near-still. Spend the spikes on transitions and hero moves, never on idle motion.
10. **Spend stillness once, then pay it off.** The longest near-still run is 1.1 s (0.27–3.07 s). Where a film holds still mid-film, it does so once, for 1.0–1.6 s (60–96 f), on the key claim, the success state or a "working" state, under a music dropout or breakdown, and keeps every other near-still stretch under 0.6–0.8 s. The biggest motion spike lands hardest right after the only stillness (a frame difference 33× the film's median), but a hold of 2.1–3.1 s before it reads as dead air.
11. **Spotlight with silence, not with effects.** Cut a statement card 3–12 f into a 12–24 f hole in the music and bring the beat back about 17 f later; give a key claim a 2–4 beat bass and kick dropout and land the next visual event on the returning downbeat; mute the sub 12–18 f before the hardest cut and release it on the cut frame. Sound details are in `references/sound.md`.
12. **Cuts lock to the music.** Hero cuts, drops and act boundaries land within ±1 f of a beat or music onset; in the tightest films 13 of 18 cuts sit within ±40 ms of the 16th grid, and phrase groups land exactly one beat apart. Ordinary cuts that drift about 110 ms off the beat, or loosen in the second half, make picture and music feel layered rather than composed. Consistent with the skill.
13. **The end card assembles, holds briefly, and stops.** It runs 2.2 s (0.9–3.9 s) when there is one. The lockup assembles over 1.0–1.3 s, then holds 0.6–1.3 s either dead still or creeping ≤ 6% (a −6% exhale, a +0.8% drift, a 1 px/f creep, or only the stage tone breathing). It ends on a hard cut to black, or the stage dims 4 f before a 15 f logo fade and about 22 f of black. A card that cuts in already complete and sits static and silent for 1.0 s reads as a dead frame, and so does a bare 2.4 s hold; a URL static for 0.23 s and a logo held for 0.35 s are too short to read. Films with no end card feel truncated.
14. **Repetition speeds up.** A repeated reveal runs about 40% faster the second time; word stagger compresses through a film (24 f per word on the title, 5 f per word by mid-film) as the viewer learns the grammar; a ratchet list accelerates (gaps of 26, 14, 12, 8, 8, 4, 4 f). Re-frame every third repetition of a cycle: start it in giant type mid-sentence and pull back to 0.6× over 50 f, or add a 3× macro insert. Five near-identical cycles turn formulaic by the fourth.
15. **Multi-cell loops are polyrhythms.** Give each cell a loop length with no small common factor (for example 244, 260, 428 and 462 f) so the whole never repeats inside the film, keep each cell's shots at 0.8–2.6 s, and keep cuts in different cells at least 6 f apart.

## 2. Smoothness

1. **Three curve families, one per role.** Films use 2–3 easing characters, assigned by role, never mixed at random:
   - **Arrivals** are expo-out: peak velocity on the first frame, half the travel in 2–8 f, velocity decaying ×0.92 per frame (×0.84–0.97; τ ≈ 12 f), 90% of the travel in about 40% of the duration and a settle tail of 18–55 f. `E.out` over 48–84 f reproduces this (its velocity decays ×0.86–0.95 per frame at those lengths).
   - **Exits** ease in, near a cubic (power ≈ 2.8–3, or ×1.14 per frame), over 10–24 f, and leave the frame or cut on the fastest frame. `E.in` is steeper (about a power-6 curve that puts 20–24% of its travel in the last frame over 24–30 f); keep it for implosions and use a cubic ease-in for exits into a cut.
   - **Repositioning moves and the camera** use either a steep in-out with the peak at 43–55% (about `cubic-bezier(0.8, 0.05, 0.07, 1.02)` for large object moves over 34–60 f, between `inOut` and `whip`; and `cubic-bezier(0.6–0.65, 0–0.1, 0.35–0.4, 1)` for camera pushes over 56–108 f, which is the skill's `smooth`), or a momentum curve for scrolls, panels and container growth: the peak at 18–35% of the move, then a long exponential tail decaying ×0.89–0.94 per frame (`cam`, `rest`). Linear appears only in drifts, data animation and luminance ramps.
2. **Zero overshoot is the norm.** Just over half of the films measure 0% overshoot on every tracked move. The rest keep large moves to 1–4% (a bezier handle of 1.01–1.05) or spend one stylised overshoot: a line scale-settling from at most 1.17×, a slam from 1.4–2.1× settling in 20–26 f, a word gap that closes 31% past rest in 8 f and relaxes in 18 f, or a +31% squash on a single 3D impact that settles in 12 f with no rebound. No film uses a spring with more than one oscillation. Consistent with the skill's damping law, and stricter in practice.
3. **One settle constant per element class.** All the type in a film settles with one constant: τ = 13–16.5 f (95% at 40–45 f), or the same 20–24 f expo-out on every word. Heavier elements settle slower: a big stat numeral travels half as far as body words (about 5% FH) over twice as long (38 f against 20 f).
4. **The duration ladder (60 fps).** Popover or menu 8–12 f; toggle fill 8–10 f; container birth 13 f; chip morph or button fill 16–20 f; button-to-panel morph 23 f; collapse or shrink 11–40 f; modal 26 f; window or sheet 36–52 f; slow container expansion 62 f; camera push or pull 56–108 f; inertial scroll to rest 76 f. The slowest move runs about 10× the fastest (the skill asks for ≥ 3×). Camera moves run 0.5–1.8 s (median about 1.0 s), the lower part of the skill's 0.6–2.4 s clamp.
5. **An exit is its entrance reversed and about 30% shorter.** A drop zone that grows in 48 f collapses in 34–38 f; a window that rises with its 56 px/f peak at 33% of the move leaves at the same 56 px/f with the peak at 68%. Consistent with the skill.
6. **Overlap is a share of the leading move.** A secondary event starts at 55–75% of the primary move, so both settle within 2 f of each other (a caption starts in the last 25% of a camera move, or within 6 f of its landing). In a layout push the neighbour moves about 0.4× the incoming panel's travel and starts about 28 f earlier. An interaction leads its effect by exactly 1 f. Colour settles 20–40 f behind the motion that caused it. The next word starts while the previous one is still dimming. Steps inside one action run back to back with 0–8 f gaps. Consistent with the skill's 20 f overlap, expressed as a share.
7. **Staggers (60 fps).** Words in a fast line 4–6 f; read-paced words 8–12 f; one word per beat 16–28 f; letters 2–3 f; siblings 4 f and rows 6–10 f; a dealt row of cards 5 f; list and stack inserts on a metronome of 14 f (or one beat). Fanfare reveals use shrinking gaps from the centre outward (12, 6, 2, 4, 4 f), so the reveal accelerates. Consistent with the skill's stagger table.
8. **Every shot is a U in speed.** It enters fast (20–45 px/f typical, 11–100), cruises slowly while the eye reads (1.5–8 px/f), accelerates over its last 10–24 f to 14–72 px/f (median about 35), and cuts on that peak. The peak-to-cruise ratio is 10–30×. Opening a shot static for 6 f after an accelerating exit loses the handoff, and it shows.
9. **Peak speeds by kind.** Text being read: ≤ 3 px/f. Camera cruise under readable text: 1.5–3 px/f. Camera push peak: 4.8–9.6% scale per frame (about 25–47 px/f of translation). Discrete objects: up to about 80 px/f (an unblurred card push reached 149 px/f). Frame-filling edges (wipes, floods, zoom-throughs, container growth, whole-frame scrolls): 150–300 px/f, up to about 450, for a few frames.
10. **Motion blur lives only on fast frames.** Nearly half of the films use no motion blur at all (apart from a cursor smear). The rest blur only moves above about 20–50 px/f (median about 40 px/f), put 10–20 px of directional blur on slams, flips and whips, and keep every settled element pin-sharp. Large UI and type stay legible and clean unblurred at 47–56 px/f. Films animated at 30 fps (or on twos) without blur strobed at 60–150 px per displayed frame, while a film animated natively at 60 fps ran 100–183 px/f peaks unblurred without strobing.
    - **Skill check:** the skill blurs anything above 12 px/f; measured unblurred moves read clean up to about 40 px/f at 60 fps, so the trigger is conservative (it costs render time, not quality).
11. **Fast transforms front-load.** A 3D flip puts 35–37% of its rotation in the first frame (blurred) and completes in 10 f. A zoom-through covers 8× in 18 f, fastest at the start. A logo scale slam shrinks at a constant 2.8–3% per frame in log space after a fast start (from over 20× to 1× in 66 f), with luminance tied to scale.
12. **Shape follows speed.** Small markers stretch with speed (up to 8× on a fall, 2.2× mid-hop); a tab pill stretches 1.6–1.9× mid-travel, with its velocity peak at 25% of a 20 f move; a dot cursor smears to 5× its length, lagging its speed by 6–10 f and recovering over about 28 f after it stops. A morph changes one dimension and locks the other (a pill collapsing into an icon keeps its height), on a symmetric expo in-out over 40 f peaking at 50%.
13. **Author at the delivery frame rate.** Up-converting from about 24 to 60 fps leaves a 5-frame judder cadence and duplicate frames that undercut premium motion. Consistent with the skill's 60 fps rule.
14. **What reads as expensive.** Zero overshoot; velocity carried across every cut; one settle constant for all type; velocity peaks early with a long exponential tail; secondary moves smaller and earlier than the hero; settled type that is crisp; a 10–30× contrast between fast moments and the reading cruise; exits that mirror entrances; stillness spent once, on purpose.
15. **What reads as cheap.** The same template twice in a row; per-letter re-centring of a centred line (visible micro-jitter); animating on twos at 30 fps; a stall between an accelerating exit and the next shot; bounce on things that merely arrive; a 2-frame effect that carries a story beat nobody can see; ambient motion on everything at once.

## 3. Timing of text

1. **Words per card.** Median 3–4 (1–7). Type-led acts never show more than 5 words at once; kinetic runs use 1–4 words per card; captions over UI carry 3–11 words, 2–3 per line, at most 4 lines. Consistent with the skill's line limits.
2. **Dwell is about 0.4 s per word.** Total on-screen time runs 0.3–0.55 s per word (median about 0.4 s) with a floor of about 0.5 s per card. A single hero word holds 0.5–1.5 s; a 2-word stat 1.2 s; a finished typed sentence 1.2–2 s after its last character; a caption over UI a fixed 2.7–3.4 s whatever its length, with 1.1–2.3 s after its last word lands. Below about 0.25 s per word nothing reads: 13 words in 0.76 s, 7 words in 0.4 s, a 3-line caption legible for 0.75 s and a URL static for 0.23 s all failed. Never invert priority: the call to action gets at least as long per word as the claim before it.
   - **Skill check:** the skill's 36 f + 6 f per word hold matches statements and captions; a rhythmic run of one-word slam cards that together form one sentence holds 32–42 f per card in total.
3. **Copy rate.** Films run 0.4–1.7 on-screen words per second, not counting UI text; type-led acts run 1.0–1.7. A 30 s stretch with no benefit line leaves viewers to infer the value from UI alone.
   - **Skill check:** the skill's launch budget of 20–35 words (about 0.5–1 word per second) sits at the low end of the measured range; it suits product-led films.
4. **Words arrive from a partial state.** They start at 40% opacity or 50% grey rather than 0, rise 6–120 px (median about 25 px), and settle in 12–24 f; contrast reaches 90% within about 8 f. Where blur is used on entry it is 4–12 px (median about 7 px) and clears early; nearly half of the films reveal words with opacity and position only.
5. **Entry recipes (60 fps).**
   - **Rise and brighten:** a rise of about 9% FH, the remaining distance shrinking ×0.84 per frame (settled in 20 f), opacity 40 → 100% over 18 f, words 4–10 f apart.
   - **In-place build:** the final line is laid out first; each word ramps opacity over 4 f, 4 f apart, with a 12–17 f pause before the key word; the whole line scale-settles from 1.17× to 1.0 (τ = 13 f). A starting scale above 1.17× reads as a zoom, not a settle.
   - **Mask slide-up:** each word rises out of a clipped line in 10–12 f and darkens from grey to ink, 8 f apart.
   - **Blur to focus:** opacity in 8 f, blur 8 → 0 px over 16–20 f, a 13 px rise over 28 f, words 20–22 f apart when each word is its own beat.
   - **Word roll:** the outgoing word rises 15–24 px with 6–8 px of blur and fades in 10 f; the incoming word starts 21–26 px low and settles in 20 f; 5–6 f per word, left to right.
   - **Slam:** the word is at full opacity on the cut frame, entering from 1.4–2.1× with 2–4 f of motion blur, and settles in 20–26 f; its accent comes from spatial overshoot (a word gap closing 31% past rest in 8 f), never from a fade.
   - **Converging lock-up:** the subject rises 70 px while the payoff drops 41 px, meeting on one baseline in 18 f, the words 2 f apart.
   - **List roller:** one line pitch (1.5× the font size) in 20 f, every 30 f; the active line's colour flips at mid-move instead of tweening.
   - **Accordion:** wide word gaps collapse with τ = 15 f, each word travelling in proportion to its index; the URL pulls into focus last, about 8 f behind its position.
   - **Tracking collapse:** letter-spacing about 3× wide collapses half-way in 2 f and settles in 24 f (the skill's 24 f matches).
6. **Lay out the final line, then reveal into it.** If a line must re-centre as words arrive, move it once per word on an expo-out over 24–38 f, or let it drift 0.5–2 px/f. Re-centring on every letter leaves visible micro-jitter.
7. **Type-on speed depends on role.** Headlines 15–22 characters/s with hard per-letter pops and no fade; product prompts 18–72 characters/s (median about 46), time-boxed to about 68 f whatever their length, on an eased count (55–75% of the characters in the first 20% of the time), then a 72–120 f read hold; streamed replies about 59 characters/s; system mono labels 22–45 characters/s; a wordmark about 11 letters/s. A long typed line scrolls left once the caret passes 88% FW, fading its head through a soft mask.
8. **Text rarely leaves on its own.** It goes with the cut while the background accelerates out (the background starts accelerating about 20 f before the cut, after the text is already still); or in two steps (dim to grey for 8–16 f, then remove); or each word rises 10–20 px and vanishes in 2–3 f, 2–4 f apart in reading order; or it backspaces (4–6 f per letter, or whole words 5–10 f apart). Caption blocks over UI fade together over 26–28 f, finished before the next camera move. A slow fade-out on a hero line never appears. Consistent with the skill.
9. **Text being read barely moves.** It travels at about 2 px/f (0–12.5) and stays sharp; once landed it tightens 2–5% over 40–50 f or drifts 1–2 px/f instead of freezing. Oversize or blurred type may travel 35–360 px/f, because nobody reads it at that moment, but it must be sharp and under about 15 px/f within 10 f and legible before 0.5 s.
10. **Recency colour, used sparingly.** The newest word or letters arrive in the accent and settle to ink over 12–50 f (median about 24 f). Applied to every word on every card, it becomes a tic by the third card. Consistent with the skill's 10–30 f.
11. **Sizes.** Statement font size 5.5–11% FH (median about 8%, about 86 px); caption cap height over UI 3.6–5% FH; standalone hero words 11–48% FH (median about 21%); end lines and URLs 4–8% FH; UI text that must be read ≥ 2.5% FH (about 27 px). Type within a film spans up to about 12× (4–48% FH).
12. **Hierarchy from colour or from weight, not both.** Either colour carries it (the active line white, inactive lines at about 30% luminance, one accent word) in a single regular weight, or weight carries it (light against semibold in one colour, with 30–50% of the words bold).

## 4. Transitions

1. **The mix.** About half of all beat changes are hard cuts. The rest are carried by continuous camera moves (about 18%), morphs (about 8%), match cuts (about 8%), light or colour floods and wipes (about 6%), and 1–2 f blends or blur dips (about 8%). A film uses 5–10 transition types. Consistent with the skill's 6–8.
2. **No crossfades.** No film crossfades between scenes. The only dissolves are 1–2 f blends (30–70% incoming) placed on a motion peak, which read as hard cuts without strobing; a 6 f blur dip that hides one cut; and an 18 f hand-off between two captions. Consistent with the skill.
3. **The velocity-matched cut is the workhorse (most of the films).** The outgoing move accelerates ×1.14 per frame (×1.07–1.24) for 10–24 f; the cut lands on its fastest frame; the incoming shot is already moving at 20–45 px/f (11–100), or covers 15–25% of its travel in its first frame, and decays ×0.92 per frame. Keep one screen direction per act and carry the velocity's sign across every cut: direction continuity replaces dissolves.
4. **Hide the cut.** Cut on the fastest frame. Cross a foreground object through frame at the camera's peak speed. Leave one blank frame (ground only) after a hard cut to reset the eye without a flash. Hide a 10× scale jump in a single frame of horizontal motion blur. Swap text on the frame of peak background velocity instead of cutting. Cut only the background (every 10–22 f) under a locked foreground to imply time passing. Pass through: the chosen object exits right in the wide and enters from the left in close-up at about 80 px/f, blurred, settling over 55 f.
5. **Scale match cuts.** If the exit shrinks, the next shot enters oversized and keeps shrinking: a label jumps 1.76× across a cut and keeps pulling back; a shrinking logo cuts to an 8× macro of the same mark that zooms out in 18 f; a word shrinks into a mark that zooms from 9× to 0.77× in 20 f and breathes back to 1.0 over 20 f; a full-frame card cuts to the same card at 77% of the frame width, still shrinking and decelerating over 36 f.
6. **Shape and colour match cuts.** Pill to circle to the logo's circle; square to a portal frame; a symbol to the logotype; an icon match-cut across a full polarity change. Keep the anchor within about 7% FW of its previous screen position. In a colour-inverted match cut, figure and ground swap around a constant anchor (scaled about 2.5×) while its drift continues across the cut.
7. **Object continuity carries the film.** One relay object binds abstract beats: a point of light that appears in 4 or more shots and causes each transformation, a progress line whose travelling dot leads the camera, a cursor dot. The product is born from the type: a caret in a word gap grows into a capsule, a bar and then the UI header over 62 f; a hairline in the gap pushes the words apart and becomes a prompt box in 13 f; a text pill grows into a table card over 36 f while its letters roll forward through the alphabet, one step every 2 f. The whole world can shrink into a point (11 f) that becomes the next object, and an object can hold its exact screen position while a new context appears behind it.
8. **Light as a seam (flat luminance and colour only, no bloom).** Ramp frame luminance linearly into the cut (+2.25 luma per frame for 28 f), cut on the downbeat into the act's brightest frame, then let it exhale about 60% over 86 f. Other measured light seams: a soft-edged bright band about a fifth of the frame wide, sweeping at about 190 px/f for 14 f; a radial flood in 6 f; a slow flood in 60 f; a linear bleach to white (+6.3 luma per frame over 26 f); an iris grown from a glyph, its radius ×1.25 per frame over 20 f; a theme flip in 6 f with the top bands leading; a portal zoom-through of 30 f on an expo-in, its peak at 80%, the opening morphing from 1:1 to 16:9 while its border leaves in 9 f.
9. **Durations (60 fps).** High-energy seams 1–20 f; morphs 13–62 f; floods 6–60 f; camera-move transitions in one-take films 44–108 f. The skill's energy table is consistent.
10. **Vary, then bookend.** The same 14 f wipe used twice 1.2 s apart, five identical prompt cycles and a camera vocabulary of one push-and-pull curve all read as presets. Close by mirroring the opening (about a third): a window leaves as the exact time-reverse of its entrance at the same peak speed, the closing pill reappears in a word gap, the same background and sound cluster return.
11. **Polarity flips are seams, not cuts.** Change stage polarity only at act boundaries and use a different device each time (a 26 f bleach, a 20 f iris, an inverse cut, an inverse match cut); never repeat one device.

## 5. Camera and depth

1. **A virtual 2D or 2.5D camera.** Most of the films move the camera only in scale and translation, plus perspective planes; a few use a 3D camera (one continuous take around a UI slab, and two abstract 3D acts). No film uses handheld shake.
2. **Holds stay alive, slowly.** A hold drifts 0.3–2.5 px/f or creeps in scale about 4% per second (0.75–9%; 3–4% over 60 f is typical). Faster than about 9% per second reads as a move, not a hold. The best demo cameras cross zero velocity smoothly instead of parking. A camera dead-locked for 1–4 s between moves, with its only life a background breathing ±15% in luminance, gives the highest near-still share of all (57%) and an energy curve that deflates after mid-film.
3. **Pushes and pulls.** A push takes 1.5–3.1× per move over 30–70 f (10–90% in about 35 f), cubic in-out with its peak at 48–55%, peaking at about 5% scale per frame (never over 10%). Pull-backs take 0.43–0.81× over 44–108 f. A two-stage push reaches 7.2× in 88 f. Put a foreground object across the frame at the peak to hide the speed.
4. **Punch-ins are cuts.** An establishing wide lasts 20–64 f, then an on-axis punch-in of 2–2.3× cuts in, with the cursor and hover state continuous across it. Every interaction gets a 3–5× close-up of 0.8–2.6 s. A wide shot of unreadable UI lasts at most about 1.7 s unless something large moves in it.
5. **Fast, slow, fast inside a shot.** Enter at 11–85 px/f, cruise at 1.5–8 px/f while text reads, exit at 15–40 px/f into the cut, and keep consecutive shots moving the same way.
6. **Roll is secondary action.** A push carries a −4° roll that swings back to level within about 25 f; pull-backs roll up to +8°, whips up to −14°. It is never a fixed dutch angle.
7. **Depth without spectacle.** Perspective planes (rotateX 25° flattening to 8°; one 40° tilt-back with a rack focus); an extruded product slab whose side faces (4–5% of its height) appear on 30–35° yaw orbits; backdrop blur of 20–40 px; progressive depth of field on one tilted page (blur bands of 0–8 px); one 3D-tilted object per act; a parallax z-stack used once. A 2.5D film stays 2.5D: no free orbits.
8. **Impossible-camera beats punctuate a fast demo.** In a high-energy film, every 2–3 s a crash zoom (4 f), a fly-over along a page laid back at about 55°, or a rack-focus whip (12 f soft, then an 8 f snap to sharp) interrupts the UI, then the film returns to calm UI.
9. **Camera and content land together.** UI events start at 55–75% of a camera move and captions in its last 25%, so camera and content settle within 2 f; the camera's own drift accelerates into the next transition so the momentum carries.
10. **Momentum scrolls.** A scroll peaks at 28–60 px/f at 19–35% of the move, eases in over 28–78 f and tails off over 70–200 f, never overshooting. An inertial flick enters at about 55 px/f and decays ×0.943 per frame to rest in 76 f. A closing S-curve scroll takes 42 f in and 48 f out, peaking at about 32 px/f.
11. **Whips hide off frame.** Follow the cursor or an object off the edge for about 12 f so the fastest travel happens out of shot, and re-enter at speed. A whip scroll eases in ×1.2 per frame over 20 f to about 196 px/f, then decays ×0.88 per frame over about 40 f and lands softly on its target.

## 6. UI in motion

1. **Scale.** Product windows fill 70–92% FW (median about 80%), often overflowing an edge. Show the hero widget alone first, then pull back for context. Scale UI to about 2× native so that body text reaches 2.5% FH. Cards in a grid are about 34% FW each. Consistent with the skill's ≥ 22 px.
2. **Legibility is the most common failure.** In most of the films the wide UI text sat at 0.5–1.5% FH (5–16 px) and read only as texture. The fixes that worked: a 2–5× push or punch-in on every interaction; and an isolate in two steps (fade the neighbours over 12 f, then dim the world over 10 f, while the hero never moves).
3. **One hero per frame.** Show 1–4 objects per frame; a dense full screen appears only as a 1–2 s establishing beat before the camera pushes into one element.
4. **Real beats stylised.** Most of the films show the real product with plausible data. Skeleton text bars, placeholder strings and unrelated stock photos read as generic; raw third-party captures read as texture.
5. **Every interaction gets a physical answer within 0.4 s.** A tab pill stretches 1.6–1.9× mid-travel (20 f, peak at 25%); a pressed card tilts about 10° over 12 f, holds 8 f, counter-swings −6° and settles by 22 f; a button press is a light envelope, its fill luminance roughly doubling each frame for 12 f, peaking for 1 f, then decaying ×0.87 per frame over 26 f (attack to release 1:2); a press with a ring runs 32–40 f.
6. **Cause and effect to the frame.** The effect starts 1 f after the interaction lands. Counters, checks and labels change on the landing frame. A label changes first and its colour fill follows 10–12 f later. Toggles flip on irregular, human gaps (4, 15, 18 f).
7. **The state vocabulary (60 fps).** Popover 8–12 f, scaling from about 5% at its anchor. Modal 26 f, growing from a 2 px dot inside a thick accent stroke that thins to a hairline as it lands. Minimise 20–22 f on an ease-in into a corner. Bottom sheet 52 f, peak at 42%. Blurred placeholder to sharp in about 20 f; skeleton to content. New elements born in the accent, settling to neutral. Row inserts on a metronome (every 14 f, or one every 40 f for a "one click" rhythm, each a 2 f line opening into an outlined row). Selection pills slide as shared elements.
8. **Numbers and data.** Counters count over 36–104 f on an ease-out with tabular figures, starting near the final value (22 units over 28 f), then hold about 24 f before anything covers them. Data draws linearly (bars about 1.6 f apart at a constant speed, a price line at a constant rate); containers, camera and logos ease.
9. **Cursor.** Just over half of the films use one; the rest show causality with light and state changes alone. Size 3.3–5.3% FH; an 11% FH cursor covers the controls it presses. Travel takes 12–16 f on an expo-out or a short in-out (velocity ×0.74–0.92 per frame, peaking at 28–80 px/f), followed by at least 60 f of hover, press and release on the target. A dot cursor may smear up to 5× its length with speed. A soft shadow offset about 40 px down and right gives a flat stage depth. A remote focus ring (a dashed ring circling the target) shows what is about to be clicked better than long cursor travel.
10. **Vary repeated interactions.** The same send-button close-up shown twice adds 2.6 s of nothing. One 3× macro insert on the decisive control per cycle is enough.
11. **Fast typing needs a hold.** Typing at 72 characters/s cannot be read live and works only with a read hold of at least 72 f afterwards.

## 7. Colour and light over time

1. **Polarity is structure.** Nearly all of the films change stage luminance or colour world by act (dark and light, or brand colour and neutral), 2–6 times per film, each change on an act boundary and riding a cut, a flip or a morph. A single inversion at about 40% of runtime, inside a hole in the music, is the strongest attention reset there is. A dark-to-bright flip on the first cut (mean luma about 50 → 205 by 0.8 s) hooks; stepping brightness down shot by shot toward the call to action lets lightness carry the story. Luma swings of 10 → 250 on every prompt-to-result cut read harsh on dark-mode phones.
2. **The accent is a signal.** Where the accent marks meaning, it averages about 5% of pixels (1–6%); the most restrained films keep 80% of frames under 1%, and the peak is about 12%. Each film has one full-bleed accent beat of about 1.2 s (or a final flood) and some retire the accent once the product appears. Monochrome films give each hero beat a single hue and keep about 84% of frames neutral. Consistent with the skill's ≤ 8% and one flood.
3. **Saturation lives off the product.** Saturated colour goes on the stage and the brand beats (at most about 30% of runtime: the open, the close and one hero beat); product UI stays under 3% saturated pixels on a near-black or near-white ground.
4. **Colour marks what just changed.** Arrivals are tinted and resolve to neutral (pins, tiles, product images, typed text) over 12–50 f, so the eye finds the change without a word.
5. **Light leads and geometry follows.** Ramp luminance linearly under eased geometry (−9 to −20 luma per frame during a morph; +2.25 luma per frame for 28 f before a hero cut); tie luminance to scale (dark when huge, white at rest); build a reveal from light values: build +50% over 14 f, dip −25% over 12 f, pop over 7–12 f with about 2% overshoot, settle in 5 f.
6. **Ambient life without movement.** The stage breathes ±15% in luminance over about 2.7 s with zero translation, or shifts its tone in place over about 100 f. Keep it to one hue family.
7. **The stage.** A near-black one step off pure (the #09090B class), cards one step lighter; flat stages free of noise.
   - **Skill check:** the skill's grain is global at 1.5–2% or absent; measured grain or dither lives only inside gradients (σ 1–2.3 levels) while flat stages stay clean.
8. **Close the colour story.** The stage dims 4 f before the mark fades over 15 f, followed by about 22 f of black; or a hard cut to black after a still of about 1.0 s. The end mark can arrive in the accent and cool to neutral over about 28 f, closing the film's colour arc.

## 8. Composition and layout

1. **One subject, centred.** The dominant layout is one hero at the frame centre: a single centred line of type at 5–8% FH cap height, or the product at 72–92% FW, often overflowing an edge.
2. **Asymmetry for heroes.** A big word left-aligned with a small line right-aligned beneath it; left-aligned ragged captions of 2–3 words per line, at most 4 lines, about 24% FW, set into the negative space that a tilted product opens.
3. **Symmetry for systems.** Mirrored columns, and centre-out reveals (a wall of bars rising from the centre, a row of cards staggering out from the middle).
4. **Negative space is designed.** Around a single type hero, 50–90% of the frame stays empty; product shots fill the frame.
5. **Crop for scale.** Giant type cut by the frame edge (a first letter cropped, a word bleeding off a corner) sells scale; a macro crop of letters at 8–10× reads as shapes before the pull-back reveals the word.
6. **Grids.** Each cell holds one idea; seams align to the pixel (a 22 px offset between rows showed whenever a cell lit up); text in a cell stays at least about 2% FH, or it is unreadable at phone size.
7. **Contrast.** Captions on a pale field at 1.4–1.6:1 vanish; keep anything that must be read at ≥ 4.5:1 (ink on white measured about 20:1).
8. **An image inside a headline.** Open the word gap over 12 f, pop the image in over 4–6 f, hold at most 16 f, then fly the words apart (accelerating ×1.22 per frame for about 16 f) while the image scales up and becomes the next shot's hero.
9. **Keep the anchor.** Across shape and colour-inverted match cuts, keep the anchor within about 7% FW of its last position; in a layout push the neighbour gives way about 0.4× as far as the incoming element.

## 9. What separates the top tier from good

1. **Momentum never dies at a cut.** Most of the films accelerate every exit into the cut and open the next shot already moving; good films ease to rest, cut, and start again from zero.
2. **The product grows out of the message.** A caret becomes the UI header in 62 f; a hairline in a word gap becomes the prompt box in 13 f; a text pill becomes a table card in 36 f. Good films cut from a title card to a screenshot.
3. **Every change is carried by something.** A relay object, a morph, a shape match or a held anchor joins the beats; a few of the films need no visible hard cut at all.
4. **Motion has one character.** 0% overshoot on every move in just over half of the films, one settle constant for all type, one curve family per role. Good films mix springs, bounces and ad-hoc eases.
5. **Stillness is spent once and paid off.** One 60–96 f near-still hold on the key claim, under a music dropout, then the biggest spike of the film; good films either never stop or stop everywhere.
6. **Speed contrast inside every shot.** A 10–30× ratio between the fast entry or exit and the reading cruise makes fast moments land and lets text read.
7. **Legibility discipline.** Text being read moves under 3 px/f and stays sharp; fast type appears only when nobody needs to read it; every UI interaction gets a push-in.
8. **Causality to the frame.** The effect follows the interaction by 1 f, state changes land on the contact frame, and UI events start at 55–75% of the camera move so camera and content settle together.
9. **Tiny interactions get mass.** A pill stretches 1.9×, a pressed card tilts 10° and counter-swings, a button fills with a 1:2 light envelope. Good films change the state in one frame or fade it.
10. **Light and polarity carry the structure.** Nearly all change stage polarity or colour world by act, with a different device each time, and keep the accent near 5% of pixels with one flood.
11. **The energy arc is designed.** An early hook peak, a 1–4 s lull at about 65%, the biggest peak at about 82%, then a short calm outro; good films peak mid-film and deflate.
12. **Repetition evolves.** A second reveal runs 40% faster, staggers compress as the film goes on, and the third cycle is re-framed; good films repeat the same template.
13. **Scale reversals hide impossible jumps.** 8–13× zoom reveals that re-frame the first frame as an object, and smash zooms hidden in one frame of blur, give the eye a single continuous move across a total change.
14. **Bookends close the grammar.** The ending mirrors the opening (the same mechanic, the time-reverse of the first move at the same peak speed, the same background) in about a third of the films.
15. **Words are performed, not displayed.** They arrive from a partial state, settle in 12–24 f on one constant, land on the beat in phrase groups, and leave with the cut rather than fading.

## 10. Common weaknesses to avoid

1. **Unreadable UI.** In most of the films wide UI text sat at 5–16 px (0.5–1.5% FH). Push in 2–5× on anything that carries meaning, or keep it out of the shot.
2. **Reads that are too short.** 13 words in 0.76 s, 7 words in 0.4 s, a 3-line caption legible for 0.75 s, a URL static for 0.23 s, a word legible for about 0.13 s. Keep at least 0.3 s per word on screen and at least 0.5 s per card, and more for the call to action.
3. **Weak endings.** No end card at all, a lockup resolving with only 0.35 s of hold, a card that cuts in complete and sits static and silent, a bare 2.4 s hold, and no call to action or URL.
4. **Repeated templates.** An identical wipe twice in 1.2 s, five near-identical prompt cycles, the same close-up twice, one push-and-pull curve for every transition.
5. **Sags.** Lower-energy stretches of 5–10 s in the middle of the demo, a 1.2 s loader with nothing happening, the slowest and most even shot placed right before the climax, and a last peak before 60% of runtime.
6. **Slow or broken hooks.** A 2.7 s still title, a 7-word question visible for 0.4 s, an opening phrase that starts mid-sentence, and a first cut into 6 f of stillness that drops the momentum.
7. **Cadence defects.** Animating on twos at 30 fps, up-converting from about 24 fps (a 5-frame judder and duplicate frames), unblurred moves of 60–150 px per displayed frame at 30 fps, and per-letter re-centring jitter.
8. **Copy errors.** Misspellings, double spaces, placeholder strings repeated on several cards, and a deliberate misspelling that read as a typo.
9. **Mixed languages.** Three or four type families in one film, and several effect styles (pixel graphics, glass, 3D bloom, flat UI) competing in one cut.
10. **Dated effects.** Glow, halos, bloom, sparkle glints, glitch slices, glass panels and multi-hue gradients appear in the weaker passages of otherwise strong films; they are the skill's Hard bans for a reason.
11. **Harsh or murky light.** Luma swings of 10 → 250 on every cut, captions on a pale field at 1.4–1.6:1, and a whole film at a mean luma of 20–30 with dense type that compression smears.
12. **A cursor in the way.** An oversized cursor covering the very button it presses, and 8–12 f popovers too fast to parse from a wide shot.
13. **Loose sync and weightless interactions.** Cuts drifting off the beat in the second half, a reveal landing in the release of the previous swell with no accent of its own, and UI clicks with no sound once the music starts. On the master, about a third clipped (true peak above 0 dBTP at −8 to −13 LUFS) and one sat at −44 LUFS, far too quiet; the skill's loudness gates exist for this.
14. **Two films in one.** A switch between two visual worlds at the act break with nothing carried across reads as a tonal jolt.
15. **Invisible story beats.** A 2-frame (33 ms) effect that carries a plot point, and a 30 s stretch with no line of benefit copy, both leave the viewer to guess.
