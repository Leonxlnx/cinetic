# Concept and story

Covers how to get from a brief to an idea worth pitching, a story device, a beat structure and an ending. Read it at Step 1 before any pixel exists, and again whenever a critic says "tour", "generic", "oversimplified" or "weak end". Numbers here are house defaults and starting points, not dogma. **A script, and a picture that never rests:** the story is told in short real sentences, one clean line at a time, centred, every line in the film's one text voice; between the lines the picture shows the product doing what the line said, with one signature shot, the product's own UI and light that moves.

## 1. What a concept is

A concept is three sentences. Write them before anything else, because every later decision (palette, easing, copy, sound) is judged against them.

| Part | Question | Tessel |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | What should the viewer believe afterwards? (≤ 8 words) | Your week doesn't fit. Tessel fits it. |
| **Device** | What one thing carries that idea through every shot? | The red "now" dot, handed from shot to shot, ending as the mark's dot. |
| **Grammar** | What rule does the film's *form* obey? | Everything fits together with no gaps; the film opens and closes on the same tick. |

What a concept is not:
- **A style.** "Bold kinetic minimal with 3D" describes execution. Two competitors could share it.
- **A feature list.** Features are proof for an idea; without the idea they are a tour.
- **A mood.** "Premium, energetic, trustworthy" is a brief for the colourist, not a film.
- **A format.** "A product walkthrough with captions" is the container.

If you can't fill the Grammar row, the film will look like everyone else's even when the idea is good. Grammar is where a film becomes ownable. The dealt direction (§3b) supplies most of it: the space, the light, the camera, the cuts, the story shape, the signature shot and the lockup.

## 2. Inventory before ideas

Ideas come from specifics. Spend a few minutes filling these lists in `TREATMENT.md` before you ideate; generic inputs produce generic films. Fill the first two rows first: they are where films made with this skill most often came up short.

| List | Prompt | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | What the name means or evokes: etymology, second meanings, the physical thing it names | The name's meaning often hands you the mark and the device. A name that means a hillside gives a rising line; a keel gives a hull below a waterline; tessellation gives pieces that tile |
| **Non-obvious behaviour** | Finish the sentence "Unlike the obvious version, it…" (it splits by item, not evenly; it ranks, not lists; it reschedules only the future) | This is the proof beat. A film that shows only the obvious version would be just as true of a simpler product |
| **Value sentence** | "Before: ___. After: ___." in the viewer's own words, no feature names | The turn of the film is the gap between the two halves |
| **Verbs** | 10 verbs the product performs, from literal (sorts, pings) to abstract (steadies, forgives) | Lens (a) mines these |
| **The product's objects** | The things on its screen, each with its states: a row that turns resolved, a card that folds, a reading that crosses a threshold, a file that locks | The device is usually one of these, because the product owns it |
| **The number** | The one quantity that changes: 412 → 1, 12 clashes → 0, 42% → 18% | A number can carry a whole film (counter as spine) |
| **Pain scene** | Time, place and what is on the screen at the worst moment | The hook is shot here |
| **Category clichés** | The five images every film in this category uses | They are banned for this film (see §4) |
| **World sound** | What the product's world sounds like: a tick, a ping, a pour, a latch | The sonic signature and the bookend |

## 3. Three lenses, and how to push past the first idea

Generate at least three raw ideas per lens (nine in all) as one-line sketches, then pick one finalist per lens. The first idea in any lens is usually the category cliché; the third is usually yours.

**Start from the smart part.** The richest source of ideas is the inventory's non-obvious behaviour: what the feature does that a naive version wouldn't, made visible. An allocation by item, with the shared costs prorated, rather than an even division of the total. A feed that ranks, where the naive one only lists. A scheduler that moves only what hasn't happened yet. Stage that difference and the viewer thinks "oh, that's smart"; stage the naive version beautifully and they think "a calculator does that". Every lens below should end up showing it.

### (a) The product's verb made literal
- **Method.** Take a verb from the list and ask what physical process it names. Stage that process using the product's own objects as the material, not an illustration beside them.
- **Push.** Make the frame obey the verb, not just an icon. Tessel doesn't show a tile animation; the whole film tessellates, and the end quilt closes its gutters into one surface.
- **Failure.** The metaphor becomes clip art (a cartoon plumb bob next to a screenshot). If the metaphor isn't built from the product's own objects or the mark, it is decoration.

### (b) The viewer's pain made visible
- **Method.** Give the pain a physical quantity (mass, height, noise, count, heat, speed, clutter) and let it grow until the frame can't hold it.
- **Push.** Let the pain break the film's form: the headline is too big for the frame, the list overflows the screen, the sound turns into a buzz. The turn then relieves the *same* quantity, so the viewer sees the before and after in one measure.
- **Failure.** A stock sad person at a desk; a problem stated in text instead of shown; a problem section longer than about 20% of the film.

### (c) A formal device
Pick one or two from the menu and marry them to a verb from (a).

| Device | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Relay object | One small thing handed from shot to shot, never lost | Launch films; anything with many shots |
| Bookend | Same image and sound first and last, with exactly one thing changed | Films whose promise is a before/after |
| One take | A single unbroken camera move (a descent, a pull-back, a track) | Products about depth, scale or flow |
| Container becomes product | The mark or the frame opens into the UI | Launches that need brand and product in one breath |
| Counter as spine | One number counts through the whole film | Products that reduce, sort or add up |
| Split-sync | Before and after side by side, locked to the same beat | Speed and effort claims |
| Rule of the frame | The film's grammar obeys the product's rule: only vertical moves, everything gets quieter, the horizon never tilts | Making a familiar story ownable |
| Scale jump | The same object at 16 px and at full frame | Products whose small unit adds up to something big |

### When all nine ideas are weak
Run these prompts, one at a time, against the inventory:
- **Make the invisible visible.** What does the product change that nobody can see: risk, moisture, time, attention, noise?
- **Follow one unit.** Pick one record (one alert, one paycheck, one line of code) and tell its life.
- **Invert.** Show the absence: the silence after the alerts stop, the empty afternoon.
- **Borrow a physical law.** Gravity, buoyancy, pressure, tessellation, magnetism. Which one is the product secretly about?
- **Make the logo guilty.** Build the mark from the problem's pieces, so the lockup is the resolution.
- **Constrain.** No cuts; no words before the halfway point; only three objects; one colour change in the whole film; only the product's own UI elements.
- **Oppose the category.** List what every film in the category looks like (a dark terminal, a shield, a glowing chip) and do the opposite on purpose.

## 3b. Directions: the deck, the idea sentence and the signature shot

Left alone, an agent makes the same film every time: keyword fragments on a plain ground, small marks, a push-in, an
end card. Short sentences, one line at a time, are right; a plain *picture* is not. The direction deck gives every film
its own visual idea before the script and the treatment exist.

**What a direction is** (`assets/directions/directions.json`, read through `scripts/deck.py`, never whole): a complete
art direction for a 6-45 s film:
- **the world:** one of four spaces (flat; layered 2.5D; perspective planes; a lit relief of extruded solids under one
  key light, with shadows cast from their real heights), the set, the depth planes;
- **the light:** one of seven kinds (a raking key, faces shaded by angle, a sun crossing a day, a travelling pool, an
  exposure event, light from an object in the scene, a material sheen), built from parts that exist
  (`references/lighting.md`);
- **the palette:** 2-3 colour recipes with ground, ink and accent proportions, in four ground tones (light, dark, mid,
  colour); every deal holds a mid or colour ground when the pool has one;
- the type settings and behaviour, the camera language, the cut grammar;
- **the story shape:** one of sixteen (problem and turn, journey, one to many, many to one, detail to whole, whole to
  detail, assembly, chain, day arc, call and response, twist, accelerando, result first, nested, loop, reveal);
- **one signature shot**, concrete (at least three quantities: frames, seconds, px, degrees, factors), with its build
  (transforms, clip paths, SVG paths, baked plates, state functions; nothing that loads the GPU on every frame), a node
  budget (at most 1,500 animated nodes) and its library entries, plus an alternative for a redraw;
- **the lockup:** one of ten kinds (the device becomes the mark, the mark as an object in the world, a pull back, the
  light finds the mark, a dead stop, a held frame, the world writes the name, everything else leaves, the mark as a
  shadow, the mark resolves), built from primitives that exist, centred and built from the film's last element (the ten
  end-card designs in `references/devices/logo-and-end-card.md`); a lockup that fades in from nowhere over a radial
  plate is refused;
- idea-sentence templates, the fit (formats, paces, assets, length), the music character and its tempo range, the
  primitives it leans on and the ones that fight it, a 4-6 beat skeleton whose speaking beats each take one sentence of
  the script, an **update skeleton** (hook, change, payoff, end) and an avoid list;
- a pre-softened foreground layer in layered spaces (one shape from the product's world, 1.2-1.6× the frame height,
  softened once in its plate, crossing near a frame edge at 1.5-2.5× the camera's speed), unless the direction turns it
  off with a reason.

**The deal.**
```
python3 scripts/deck.py --brief "<one line>" --format <launch|update|...> --pace <calm|crisp> \
        [--have ui,input,photos,footage,audio,number,name] [--seconds N] [--changes 2-4] [--brand brand/brand.json] --options 3
python3 scripts/deck.py --brief "<one line>" --format <fmt> --direction <id>     # the sheet: out/direction.json, out/DIRECTION.md
python3 scripts/deck.py --show [<id>]        python3 scripts/deck.py --redraw palette|type|signature|direction --reason "..."
```
- `--have` names what really exists (product UI, an input the product is used with, pictures worth building on,
  supplied footage, a real recorded clip, one honest number, a name whose meaning the film can use); directions built
  on an asset fit only when it exists.
- A deal of three always differs in camera motion, signature, story shape and lockup; spans three kinds of space and
  two kinds of light; holds one rich direction whenever one fits, and at most one quiet one.
- The seed comes from the brief, so the same brief deals the same three; a small history deals the directions of the
  last films less often (`--no-history` to skip it).
- `--keep palette,type` when the brand fixes colours or type; `--brand brand/brand.json` maps the dealt roles onto the
  brand (`references/brand-kits.md` §6).
- Redraw a slot only when it contradicts the brand, needs an asset that does not exist, or cannot carry the idea;
  `--reason` is required, each slot at most twice, every redraw logged in the sheet.
- Text first: the deal favours type-led directions (the line itself is the shot) x4 and graphic ones, made in code on
  clean grounds, x1.5. Imagery-led directions (`assets/directions/imagery.json`) are opt-in: dealt only with `--have
  photos` (the product's own pictures, or a set worth building on) or `footage`, because sloppy stock is worse than
  none (`references/imagery.md` §8).

**Choose by the idea.** Hold each direction against the inventory: which way of making the picture can carry something
only this product has? Choose that one, not the most familiar, and write one line on why. Then adopt it: do not blend
it with the film you would have made anyway.

**The idea sentence and the competitor test.**

> In this film, **[something only this product has]** becomes **[the device this direction calls for]**.

- The first blank comes from the inventory (the name's meaning, the non-obvious behaviour, the product's own objects
  or its number), never from the category ("speed", "AI", "simplicity"). The second comes from the sheet's templates.
- **Competitor test:** put the nearest real competitor in the sentence. If it still works, the idea belongs to the
  category, not to this product; go back to the inventory. "A field of screens to fly into" works for every app; "the
  412 alerts the product folds into one card become planes the camera flies through until only one is left" does not.
- Both go into `TREATMENT.md` under the direction's name and seed, and into `out/direction.json`, where the critics read
  them.

**One signature shot; the picture never rests.** The signature is the one shot a viewer will ask "how was that made"
about. It happens once, on the beat the skeleton marks; the film may echo it (smaller, at the open, or inside the
lockup), never repeat it, and no beat uses the alternative. Every direction states its life inside a shot (creep,
drift, lean, orbit, a key that never stops moving) and a light event, and the render checks hold the floor against a
flat film: the camera or the ground moving on 90 % of body frames, two depth planes in half the shots, 40 % of seams
carried, a designed moment at least every 3 s, one light event per act, a clear scale change on 30 % of cuts
(`references/checks.md` §6).

**Concepts inside the direction.** The three lenses (§3) still run, but the direction supplies the concept's Grammar
row: the lenses find the idea; the direction decides how the film looks and moves. Then write the script
(`references/copy-and-type.md` §1-2b: 6-9 short sentences for a launch film, one line at a time), draw the devices and
one text animation per line with `pick.py --lines <n>`, and write the treatment on the dealt skeleton: beats, each
sentence with its text animation, device path, the signature beat, the lockup.

**Devices, with variety.** A device per beat (about 1.5-2.5 s), drawn with `pick.py` and read in the device book
(`references/devices/INDEX.md`), from many families; one device everywhere is a template unless it is the film's
identity. Mentioning one device in a brief does not make it the whole film.

## 4. Tests that kill weak ideas

Run every finalist through these. A concept that fails the ownership or deletion test is out, however pretty.

| Test | How to run it | It fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Swap in a competitor's name and logo. Then list every prop in the film and strike any that could appear in another product's film. | The film still works with another name, or fewer than two owned props remain |
| **Deletion** | Delete the demo beats: the value must still read. Then delete the value beats: the film must stop working. | The value vanishes with the demo, or the film still "works" without the value (it was a tour); in a chapters film, a chapter shows no result or no through-line carries across them |
| **Muted** | Describe the film using pictures only: no copy, no sound. | The problem → result can't be told from the images |
| **Feature word** | Would a stranger, watching muted, use the brief's own word for the feature ("streak", "split", "cache hit")? | They would describe a neighbouring thing (a chart of pages instead of a streak). The device serves the feature; when a name-derived metaphor makes the feature read as something else, keep the feature's familiar form and let the device dress it |
| **Poster** | Describe the final frame as a still printed on a wall. | It doesn't say who made it and what it does |
| **One sentence** | Say the idea aloud in one sentence. | You need "and" (two ideas) or an adjective (a style) |
| **Retell** | How would a viewer describe it to a friend tomorrow? | The answer is a style ("it was slick") rather than an image ("the dot that…") |
| **Specificity** | Imagine a simpler product or feature (an even split instead of an itemised one, a list instead of a ranking). Would the film be just as true of it? | Yes: the proof shows the naive version. Rebuild the proof around the non-obvious behaviour |
| **Budget** | Count capabilities and beats against the format row in `SKILL.md` §1. | It needs more than 2 capabilities, or more than 8 beats per 30 s (10 in crisp) |
| **Truth** | Does the product actually do what the picture shows? Do the numbers and states on screen agree with the story? | The film promises a behaviour the product lacks, or a frame contradicts the story: the person who paid is shown owing, a "sorted" list isn't sorted, a ratio doesn't match the numbers beside it |

**Generic props.** These fail ownership unless the product literally is the thing: rocket, lightbulb, globe with arcs, up-and-right chart, a phone or laptop floating in a void, a glowing orb or sparkle for AI, shield or padlock for security, puzzle pieces for integration, gears for automation, a stopwatch for speed, a magnifying glass for search, confetti for success, a handshake, a bullseye.

**Score the survivors** 0–3 on ownership, muted clarity, device strength, surprise and buildability. Pick the highest total with ownership ≥ 2; break ties toward buildability, because an idea you can finish beats one you can't.

## 5. Choose, graft, pitch

- **Graft.** Keep the winner's spine and take the single best element of each loser: a grammar, a line, an opening image. Name what you grafted in the treatment so it doesn't creep back as a second idea.
- **Logline.** Write it as the film seen, in ≤ 15 words: *device + what it does + the turn + the result*. "A red now-dot draws a week that doesn't fit, then fits it."
- **Pitch.** When the user wants to choose, show three loglines, each with its device and one frame description, plus your recommendation and why, in ≤ 12 lines. Otherwise choose, record the other two in `TREATMENT.md`, and move on.

## 6. Story-device design

The device is the film's continuity and the reason it reads with the sound off. Design it as carefully as the mark, because it will become part of the mark.

**Where devices come from**, in order of preference:
1. the product's own specific object, with its states: the deposit row, the incident card, the moisture reading, the changed brick in a build graph;
2. the name's meaning: the plumb bob, the keel, the rising line of a hillside;
3. the key number;
4. a primitive of the mark;
5. the accent colour itself;
6. a sound (only as a partner to a visual device).

A bare dot, line or block is the lazy default. It is also Tessel's device (the red now-dot) and the starter's (the dot that becomes the mark), so reaching for it pulls your film toward theirs. Use one only when the concept truly needs it, and then give it behaviour only this product has: the dot that is the calendar's "now" and can't move into the past, not a dot that travels because devices travel.

**Properties of a good device**
- **One.** A single instance at a time. Two devices split attention.
- **Able to change scale.** Small enough at rest to travel through any shot (a row, a card or a chip travels as well as a dot does), and able to fill the frame once, as a flood, an iris or a push into it.
- **Has a state that changes.** It ticks, fills, drops, locks or counts. A device that only moves is a logo on a tour.
- **Owned.** It passes the ownership test on its own.
- **Ends as the mark**, or as one piece of it, in a launch film or sting (ideally, not by force). In a feature loop or product video it is the unit the product acts on (the card, the row, the file) and ends in its resolved state.

**Designing the path.** Write the path as a chain of "becomes", one link per shot, and name the seam type for each link (the seam catalogue is in `references/transitions.md`). Tessel's chain:

> full-frame red iris → ticking dot → the mark's dot → the app's now dot → the send button → red flood → the now line → the period in "Everything fits." → the mark's dot → centre frame

Rules for the path:
- **It never blinks out.** Full opacity through every seam, even for "clean" shots. Four missing frames at a cut read as a mistake.
- **It changes role, not style, at a seam.** Size, colour and edge quality match across the cut; the role (marker, button, period) changes on the next beat.
- **The eye is already there.** At every cut the device sits where the viewer is looking, so the cut is a handoff, not a search.
- **One flood.** The device fills the frame at most once; that flood is usually the turn.
- **Lock the target before landing.** A device landing on a moving word collides with it; the word locks first, then the device lands.

**Bookends.** Open and close on the same image and sound with exactly one thing changed; that change is the promise. Tessel ends on the tick-tock it began with. A clock reading 3:12 at the start and 3:13 at the end says "fixed in a minute" without a word.

**Device failures:** too big to travel (a full dashboard can't become a period); decorative (it moves but never acts); generic (a dot or a line that any product could use); lost in the middle act because the demo "needed the space"; replaced by a second device for the ending.

## 7. Beats, and cause → action → result

| Beat | Job | Typical length | It works when | It fails as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Put the viewer inside the problem, already moving at frame 0 | 1–2 s | the problem is felt by 1 s while the first line says what the product is by 3 s | a logo, "Introducing…", a question, a blank frame |
| **Problem** | Escalate the pain as a visible quantity | 2–4 s | a stranger could name the pain muted | a statement with no picture under it |
| **Turn** | The product or device intervenes; the film's biggest change so far, on a downbeat | 1–2 s | it is the strongest visual event yet (flood, drop, implosion, silence then drop) | the product simply appears |
| **Proof** | One capability as cause → action → result | 4–8 s each, 1–2 in a film | the result holds ≥ 36 f before the camera leaves | a feature list |
| **Promise** | The idea stated once, as a callback to the setup words | 2–4 s | the line reuses a word from the hook | a slogan unrelated to what we saw |
| **Lockup** | The device becomes the mark; name; URL if needed | 3–5 s with tail | the final frame works as the poster | a replayed reveal, a fade to black |

Every beat that speaks carries one sentence of the script: the hook a claim from inside the problem, the problem a
claim that makes it worse, the turn a turn, the proof a claim the picture proves on the same beat, the promise the
payoff, best an echo of the hook.

**Cause → action → result**, for every product action on screen:
- **Cause.** Something visible makes the action necessary: a typed intent, an arriving event, an accumulated mess, a threshold crossed. A UI block that moves because the timeline said so reads as a screensaver.
- **Action.** The product's real behaviour, shown in its own UI (see `references/product-ui.md`). No sparkle standing in for "magic".
- **Result.** A changed state that is still true when paused: a counter that counted during the action, a list that is now in order, a line now highlighted. Hold it at least for the reading hold of any label (max(52, 36 + 7 f per word)) before the camera moves on.

## 8. Arcs by format

Production recipes (act templates, loop seams, stings, social layouts) are in `references/formats.md`; bar arithmetic is in `references/timing-grid.md`. This table is the story shape.

| Format | Arc | Device's job | Where the idea lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch film, 20–45 s | hook and what it is → problem → turn → proof ×1–2 → promise → lockup | carries every shot, becomes the mark | the turn at ~20–25% of the film, the callback at the promise |
| Launch film in chapters, 20–45 s | hook → turn (the product named) → 3–4 chapters, each a 1–3 word flash → the UI doing it → its result → promise → lockup | links the chapters (the mark, the agent's cursor, a recurring line stem) and becomes the mark | each chapter's result; the refrain at the promise |
| Product / feature video, 8–30 s | situation → cause → action → result (held) → name | it is the unit the product acts on | the result |
| Feature loop, 4–15 s | rest A → cause → action → result → rest A | its state *is* the loop | mid-loop; the last frame is the first |
| Logo sting, 3–8 s | gather → assemble with a contact → hit → wordmark → building hold | the primitives are the device | the hit |
| UI walkthrough, 20–90 s | cold open on the end result (2–4 s) → chapters, each cause → action → result → recap image → lockup | one record followed through every chapter | each chapter's result |
| Teaser, ≤ 15 s | what it is over the effect → the name and a date | a partial glimpse of the mark | the name |
| Before/after | the same shot twice with an identical camera move: without, then with | the camera move itself | the difference |

Proportions that work for a 30 s launch film: hook + problem ~20%, turn ~10%, proof ~35–40%, promise ~15%, lockup and tail ~15–20%. Write the script into those beats, one sentence a beat (5–8 in a 30 s film), and give each line what the picture does while it is read and right after: the line lands, the product does exactly that, and the next line moves on (`references/copy-and-type.md` §2b). Tessel: problem 0–6 s, turn 6–10 s, proof 10–24 s, promise and lockup 24–33 s.

**Two launch shapes; pick the one that fits, or mix them.** One unfolding story suits a product with one sharp capability. **Chapters** suit a launch with several things to show (a texting agent doing four tasks, each inside its own app; an AI receptionist set up in three flashes, then a live call): a chapter is a short flash of text (a verb, the feature in 1–3 words, or a refrain's stem: "Tessel plans." → "Tessel moves."), then the UI doing it with a concrete result, then a hand-over into the next. Vary their lengths (2–5 s) and give the strongest the 70–85 % slot. Chapters are not a tour as long as every chapter shows a result and one through-line (the device, the agent, the refrain) carries across them all.

**Loops need a cyclic story.** Don't build enter → hold → exit; build a process whose end state is its start state (a line that dries and is watered, an inbox that clears and refills). Put both sides of the seam at rest, or at the same velocity.

## 9. End cards

1. **Centred, built from the last element.** The lockup sits centred in the frame and arrives from the story, not as a new logo animation: the last picture's element becomes the mark, the last word becomes the wordmark, the film's travelling element becomes the mark's accent (the ten designs in `references/devices/logo-and-end-card.md`). It may echo the signature shot (smaller, inside the lockup) but never replays it. The name appears once.
2. **Build, then hold 2–3 s.** The lockup builds in 0.8–1.6 s, then holds 2–3 s from the frame the name and the fact are read, creeping 2–6 % (a hold past ~1.3 s visibly builds: a 10–20% dolly or the mark's creep). A card that cuts in complete and sits frozen reads as dead.
3. **URL or call to action.** ≥ 1.7 s fully readable, set at the secondary size (44–56 px at 1080p) and still while it is being read.
4. **The final frame is the poster.** It must contain the mark and the name. Check it: export the last frame, view it 480 px wide, and ask whether a stranger could say whose film it was.
5. **Tail.** At least 60 f after the last event, under the resolved lockup, while the audio decays to digital silence. Never fade to black: the poster would be black and the player freezes on it.
6. **The bookend trade-off.** A final frame of only the device (the dot alone, centred) is elegant, but it loses recall on autoplay players that freeze on the last frame and in thumbnails. Prefer playing the bookend *inside* the lockup (the dot ticks within the mark). If you must end on the device alone, keep that to the last ≤ 30 f, export the poster from the lockup frame instead, and say so in the README.
7. **Nothing else.** No row of social icons, no store badges, no "Available now" unless the brief asks; no second tagline under the first.
8. **Sound.** The ending subtracts: the music stops or filters down as the logo forms, then one soft resolved element on the wordmark rings under the hold. The end card sits well under the film's peak, which is the drop on the hero reveal; a sting is the exception (see `references/sound.md` §5.3).

## 10. Four worked concepts

All four products are invented for this file. They are chosen to be unlike each other, and unlike a calendar, so the method shows rather than the answer. Their devices differ in kind on purpose: an object from the name (the bob), a line (the waterline), a product object with states (the incident card), and one record (the deposit row).

### Plumb: a launch film (30 s)
Code review that finds the few lines in a huge pull request that can actually break production.
- **Inventory.** Name: a plumb line, a weight on a string, true vertical; "to plumb the depths". Non-obvious behaviour: unlike a linter that flags every line, it ranks the 2,184 changed lines and surfaces the 3 that can break production. Verbs: drop, sound, weigh, stop, point. Objects: diff line, gutter, line number, review box, Approve button. Clichés to ban: green-on-black terminal, code rain, bug icons, shields, rockets.
- **(a) Verb:** a steel plumb bob drops through the diff streaming past like rock strata and stops dead on line 1,284. **(b) Pain:** 2,184 lines blur into grey texture under a tired scroll; "Looks good to me." is typed; freeze; zoom into the blur to the outage. **(c) Formal:** one unbroken vertical descent through one diff; the line number is the only type.
- **Tests.** (b) fails ownership: any review tool could run it. (a) and (c) pass.
- **Chosen:** (a) as the spine, (c)'s single descent as the grammar (everything in the film moves vertically, under gravity, until the lockup), and (b)'s "Looks good to me." as the setup line, repeated word for word at the end when it has become true.
- The full treatment is the filled example in `assets/TREATMENT.md`.

### Loam: a feature loop (8 s, muted, landing page)
A soil sensor and app that tells you when a houseplant actually needs water.
- **Inventory.** Name: loam, the soil itself. Non-obvious behaviour: unlike a watering schedule, it waits for this pot's soil to reach this plant's threshold. Verbs: sense, dry, drink, tell, wait. Objects: the moisture reading, the sensor stake, the plant card, the "Water now" chip. Number: 42% → 18%. Pain scene: yellow leaves from watering on a schedule. Clichés: a watering can, a sprouting seedling time-lapse, a leaf icon, a smiling plant.
- **(a) Verb:** the soil "tells": the reading rises out of the pot as a line. **(b) Pain:** a calendar reminder waters an already-wet pot and the waterline overflows. **(c) Formal:** one cross-section of one pot fills the frame, and the waterline in the soil is the only thing that moves.
- **Tests.** (b) is negative and needs a before; a loop has no room. (c) passes muted and is cyclic by nature.
- **Chosen:** (c) with (a)'s idea that the soil speaks. The waterline is the device: it drifts down as the soil dries (from rest, eased in), touches the 18% tick, becomes the progress bar in the plant card that rises from the lower edge, and underlines "Water now" in the accent. Water arrives; the line climbs back to 42% and settles at rest, which is frame 0.
- **Script (2 lines).** "This pot needs water now." → "This pot can wait now." Same grammar twice: the loop is the callback.

### Hush: a product video (20 s)
An on-call tool that folds an alert storm into one incident and wakes one person.
- **Inventory.** Name: hush, the quiet after the noise. Non-obvious behaviour: unlike a mute button, it doesn't silence the storm; it groups 412 alerts by their shared cause and pages the one person who owns that service. Verbs: fold, group, quiet, route, wake. Objects: alert row, severity pip, incident card, lock-screen notification. Number: 412 → 1. Pain scene: 3:12 a.m., a phone that won't stop. Clichés: sirens, flashing red, a nightstand in the dark, a person bolting upright.
- **(a) Verb:** 412 alert rows fold like a paper fan into one card. **(b) Pain:** a lock screen stacks notifications faster than they can be read until the phone is a solid slab. **(c) Formal:** counter as spine, plus a bookend on the clock.
- **Tests.** The nightstand shot fails ownership, but the lock screen stays as the hook image. (a) + (c) pass muted: the number goes down as the pile folds.
- **Chosen:** (a) + (c), with a rule of the frame: after the turn the film only gets quieter. The element count, motion speed and sound density all fall until one thing moves. The device is the incident card, a product object with states. It is born as the first alert row; the other 411 fold into it as a visible stack (the count on its edge ticks down as each lands), it gains its owner's name when grouping finishes, and it slides into the lock screen as the one notification that wakes one person. Its "handled" state (a quiet sage) is the accent, and the card's folded edge is the mark.
- **Script (5 lines).** "Hush turns alert storms into one incident." / "It finds the cause 412 alerts share." / "It wakes one person, not ten." / "By 3:13, your phone is quiet." / "Hush is out now." The clock is the picture's bookend (3:12 on the lock screen under the first line, 3:13 under the fourth); one digit changed is the whole promise.

### Keel: a UI walkthrough (45 s)
A money app that keeps a buffer below the waterline of your balance, so a surprise bill never tips you over.
- **Inventory.** Name: a keel keeps a boat upright; hulls are painted red below the waterline. Non-obvious behaviour: unlike a savings pot, it fills the buffer first from every paycheck, so a surprise bill drains the buffer and never the spending money. Verbs: set aside, top up, steady, absorb, refill. Objects: the deposit row, the balance bar, the buffer under its baseline, the bill card. Numbers: a $480 buffer, a $380 repair, $1,350 of spending money. Clichés: piggy banks, stacks of coins, up-and-right charts, a person smiling at a phone.
- **(a) Verb:** the balance as a hull on a horizon, rocked by a bill and righting itself. That is an illustration world, not the UI, so it fails truth. **(b) Pain:** a $380 repair lands two days before rent and the balance dips below zero. **(c) Formal:** follow one paycheck through every chapter.
- **Chosen:** (c) with (a)'s rule of the frame: the camera may tilt when a bill hits, but the balance baseline never does. The device is the one deposit row. It lands, pours first into the buffer below the baseline, drawn in the accent (hull red, meaning "below the line: set aside"), and the rest rises above the line as spending money. (b)'s repair is chapter 3: it drains the buffer, and the part above the line never moves.
- **Script (7 lines: what it is, one per chapter, the close).** "Keel keeps a buffer under your balance." / "Every paycheck fills the buffer first." / "A $380 repair lands Tuesday." / "The buffer takes the hit." / "Spending money stays spending money." / "Payday fills the buffer back up." / "Keel is out now." The fifth line's repetition *is* the proof: nothing touched it.
