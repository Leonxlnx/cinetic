# Taste and slop: the tell catalogue

The full list of things that make motion design look cheap, templated or generated, one line each with its fix. Read it at Step 2 before the style stills, then search it during every review round. Numbers are house defaults at 1920×1080 and 60 fps, with normal ranges.

**How to use it**
- **The hard bans come first** (HB1–HB11 below). A hard-ban violation on any frame is a P0 in review, unless `BRIEF.md` lists it as brand-supplied.
- **When you invent, every entry applies.** When the user supplies a brand (colours, typeface, logo, footage, voice), the brand wins over any entry about that element, hard bans included, and each such exception is written in `BRIEF.md`; the rest still apply.
- **Cite the ID.** Critics put the ID at the start of an issue's `problem` field ("F4: expo-out fade-out on the lockup, 55% gone in 1 f"), so fixes can be traced and repeat offenders counted.
- **Search by group**, for example `grep -n '^- \*\*F' references/taste-and-slop.md` for motion, or by word (`grep -n -i 'glow'`).
- **Look for the fix, not just the tell.** Each fix names a number or a file to check it with.

**Groups:** [HB Hard bans](#hard-bans) · [A Concept](#a-concept-and-story) · [B Copy](#b-copy) · [C Type](#c-type) · [D Colour](#d-colour-and-surface) · [E Layout](#e-layout-and-framing) · [F Motion](#f-motion-and-easing) · [G Transitions](#g-transitions) · [H Camera](#h-camera) · [I UI](#i-ui-depiction) · [J Sound](#j-sound) · [K Pacing](#k-pacing) · [L Finishing](#l-finishing)

## Hard bans

These mirror `SKILL.md` "Hard bans". When you invent the look, none of them appears on any frame. `scripts/lint-film.mjs` catches what it can in the code (rule names in brackets), and the critics check the frames for the rest.
- **HB1 Eyebrow or kicker labels** above a headline ("NEW FEATURE", "01 / SPEED"). **Fix:** delete them; hierarchy comes from size and order of appearance.
- **HB2 Stacked taglines, and "Introducing…".** A tagline over or under a headline; a second statement under the first; any "Introducing". **Fix:** one line at a time, opening inside the problem (`references/copy-and-type.md` §2b, §4).
- **HB3 Text walls and filler text.** Two statements readable at once, paragraphs, bullet lists; decorative mono captions, fake metrics, version strings, labels nobody needs, lorem ipsum. **Fix:** every word is counted, held and true on its paused frame, or it goes.
- **HB4 Serif and italic.** Serif typefaces; italic or oblique styles, including a skew that fakes one [`serif`, `italic`]. **Fix:** one clean, modern sans chosen for the personality, plus a mono if needed; emphasis from size or motion (`references/brand-and-color.md` §2). The one exception is an italic accent face on a payoff word, marked `cinetic:accent-face <family>` (`references/copy-and-type.md` §6).
- **HB5 Orange, amber, beige, cream, tan or sand**, as an accent, a paper or a glow [`banned-color`]. **Fix:** an accent from red, green, teal, blue or yellow; a neutral or cool paper (the OKLCH regions are in `references/brand-and-color.md` §8).
- **HB6 Neon, and glow as decoration.** Very bright, saturated accents; halos and bloom around elements and type [`banned-color`, `glow`]. **Fix:** lower the lightness or chroma; shadows in the ink or ground's hue from one key. Light and glow that are part of a device (a tile lit from beneath, a floor glow, a frame that blooms into light) are light, built from baked plates: `references/lighting.md` §5.
- **HB7 Purple, violet or indigo**, and the purple-to-blue gradient [`banned-color`]. **Fix:** an accent from an allowed family with a written meaning. Multi-hue gradients, rainbow and spectral accents are allowed as devices that do a job (one dominant spectral signal per film, every gradient baked and dithered, `gradient-shape` outside a device), never as a decorative wash.
- **HB8 Glassmorphism as a UI style.** Frosted panels with a 10–20% white fill and a 1 px white border as the look of the product [`glass`]. **Fix:** opaque surfaces lit by the key. Glass as a material device (frost from two copies, refraction, fluted glass) is allowed where it does a job, built without `backdrop-filter` (`references/devices/INDEX.md`, glass and refraction).
- **HB9 Emoji and stock icons** [`emoji`, `stock-decoration`]. **Fix:** draw what the product itself shows; icons only where the real product UI has them.
- **HB10 Effects standing in for ideas.** Sparkles for "AI", particles, confetti, lens flares, code rain [`stock-decoration`, `emoji`]. **Fix:** the product's real behaviour, shown; every element names its job.
- **HB11 Bouncy overshoot on anything that isn't landing**, any overshoot on copy, and letters that move one by one outside a line's own text animation. **Fix:** everything eased or critically damped; overshoot of 1–7% only on a true landing, an object arriving at a surface or a lock, at most 2 per film (F3); type settles on an ease or a critically damped spring (F11).

## A. Concept and story
- **A1 Feature tour.** Feature, feature, feature, logo; the viewer has nothing to remember. **Fix:** problem → turn → promise; run the deletion test both ways (`references/concept-and-story.md` §4).
- **A2 Wrong first frame.** Opening on "Introducing…", a question, the logo or a blank frame. **Fix:** the picture opens inside the problem (felt by 1 s) while the first line says what the product is, in plain words, by 3 s (`copy-check.py`).
- **A3 Borrowed props.** A rocket, lightbulb, globe with arcs, up-and-right chart, floating phone, sparkle for AI (HB10), shield for security. **Fix:** the ownership test; build every image from the product's own objects.
- **A4 A style posing as an idea.** "Bold, kinetic, minimal, 3D." **Fix:** write Idea / Device / Grammar first; the style follows from them.
- **A5 Demo without a cause, or a rushed result.** UI moves because the timeline says so, or the result is on screen 11 f before a whip. **Fix:** cause → action → result, with the result held for its reading hold (max(52, 36 + 7 f per word) in calm, max(36, 28 + 6 f per word) in crisp).
- **A6 The device drops out.** It blinks out for 4 f at a cut, or changes size or colour across a seam. **Fix:** full opacity through every seam; hand it over on a shared prop.
- **A7 Frames that contradict the promise.** A strike-through over the tagline, overlapping tiles in an "everything fits" shot, success in the error colour. **Fix:** read every key frame literally, as a paused still.
- **A8 A recycled or dead end card.** It replays the mid-film reveal, sits static for 2.7 s, fades to black, or the last frame shows no brand. **Fix:** the device becomes the mark; a 1.5–2 s hold that builds 10–20%; the final frame is the poster.
- **A9 An oversimplified feature.** The film would be just as true of a dumber product: an even split for an itemised one, a list for a ranking. **Fix:** the specificity test; the proof shows the non-obvious behaviour (`references/concept-and-story.md` §3–4).
- **A10 An anonymous product.** The feature is never named and nothing on screen says it is software: a clever behaviour on floating cards that a stranger can't name or search for. **Fix:** the feature's name once, as a UI label, the one line or the end card, plus enough real app chrome to read as an app (`references/product-ui.md` §1).
- **A11 An end card without the brief's fact.** The brief says "shipping next week", "out now" or gives a URL, and the film ends on the name alone. **Fix:** that one fact at the secondary size under the lockup, readable ≥ 1.7 s; it is the only line besides the name (`references/formats.md` §2).

## B. Copy
- **B1 Landing-page grammar.** Eyebrow or kicker labels and stacked taglines (HB1, HB2), all-caps micro labels. **Fix:** delete them; hierarchy comes from size and order of appearance.
- **B2 Stock phrasing and punctuation.** Seamless, unlock, supercharge, AI-powered, next-gen, "the future of"; exclamation marks, emoji (HB9), rhetorical questions. **Fix:** callback copy built from the film's own words; statements end in a period (`references/copy-and-type.md` §3–4).
- **B3 A feature name as a headline.** "Smart Split." **Fix:** say what changes for the viewer in one sentence of 2–7 words ("Every bill splits itself by item.").
- **B4 Text wall.** Two lines readable at once (a declared swap excepted), more than 8 words of copy visible on one frame (10 in a crisp film), or a list with readable neighbours (HB3). **Fix:** one line at a time; a list becomes one sentence with a rotating slot, neighbours masked to unreadable slivers (`references/copy-and-type.md` §2b). A held line of up to 7 words is not a wall.
- **B5 Over budget.** More than 8 lines in a 30 s launch film (50 words at most), a line over 7 words, or more than 12 words resolving in any 5 s. **Fix:** cut adjectives first, then whole lines; let the picture carry the words you cut.
- **B6 Microcopy asked to carry meaning.** A 14–16 px toast readable for 6 f. **Fix:** ≥ 22 px on screen and held for its reading time, or treat it as texture that carries nothing.
- **B7 Proofing bugs.** "1 clashes", mixed brand casing, straight quotes, lorem, "John Doe", example domains. **Fix:** the plural helper and the string audit (`references/copy-and-type.md` §5).
- **B8 Sentence dump.** A line of more than 7 words, or a sentence revealed as one block (7 words resolving in 32 f), or two lines arriving together. **Fix:** two sentences, one line each, or one line built word by word in the film's voice (`build`, `develop`, a chain in build mode; `references/copy-and-type.md` §2b, §8); `grid-check.ts` flags it as `script` or `reveal-rate`, two lines arriving together as `read-first`, and a line built in beats with no picture between its parts as `chain-gap` or `chain-beat`.
- **B9 Subtitle film.** Lines on more than about 85% of the frames, or no text-free stretch of 1.5 s in 10 s (1 s in crisp), or lines that only sit over the picture with no animation of their own: the picture has become a backdrop for captions. **Fix:** give the product stretches with no line, and give each line its own beat alone on a clean ground before or after the picture (line, then picture), built in the film's one voice (`grid-check.ts` `text-share`, `beats`).
- **B10 Keyword pile.** Lines of one or two words that say nothing ("Speed." "Less noise." "Total control."), labels instead of claims, a run of single words in one place: the film tells no story with the picture off. **Fix:** write the script: 5–8 short real sentences, each a claim, a turn or a payoff, read in order like a story (`references/copy-and-type.md` §1–2a); `grid-check.ts` flags it as `script`.

## C. Type
- **C1 Generated-look faces.** Serif or italic as instant "elegance" (HB4), or a condensed display face for "impact". **Fix:** one clean sans from the face set by look (`scripts/faces.json`, `references/copy-and-type.md` §6), never a rounded, techno or condensed face picked for character; emphasis from size, weight contrast or motion.
- **C2 An unchosen default, or a fallback.** A ubiquitous UI default family picked by nobody, or a fallback face visible on any frame. **Fix:** choose the family on purpose, ship it locally (`@fontsource-variable/*`), gate it with `FontGate`.
- **C3 Near-miss tokens.** Display weights 590, 600 and 620, or tracking −0.04, −0.05 and −0.06, across scenes. **Fix:** one display token (for example 600 / −0.045 em), reused everywhere.
- **C4 Flat scale.** Everything at 48–64 px. **Fix:** statements 88–128 px, emphasis 1.5–1.7× that, headline to secondary ≥ 2:1.
- **C5 Faux italics from 3D shear.** Roll applied before tilt leans all UI text by ~18°, an accidental HB4. **Fix:** write `perspective() rotateZ() rotateX()` so the roll stays rigid; total shear ≤ 7°.
- **C6 Glyph collisions.** Overshoot closes a word space ("Everythingfits"), a period hangs 13 px under the baseline, a cover leaks glyph tops. **Fix:** clamp at the lock; place with `inkBox` and `baselineOf` after fonts load; pad covers by 8 px.
- **C7 Blur-dissolve on every word.** Or readable text left blurred for more than 6 f. **Fix:** blur only as one line's own text animation (`typeFocus`, a focus pull that lands sharp), never on every line; ≤ 4 px when the brand is soft, and in the crisp profile statements 6–8 → 0 px and body lines 4–6 → 0 px, always through `blurIn` so it clears by 60% of the move; land sharp.
- **C8 Jittering numbers.** Counters whose width changes, or mono timestamps in a consumer UI. **Fix:** tabular figures in the one family; digits roll 6–8 f each.
- **C9 Letter gimmicks.** Per-letter bounce, a scramble or decode laid over a line as decoration, the same letter trick on every line, wide-tracked titles at rest. **Fix:** letters move only inside a line's own text animation (a `TypeLine` preset such as `decode`, `assemble` or `swarm`, one line each, never with overshoot), collapsing to normal tracking.
- **C10 A reveal that misspells.** A letter or sweep reveal passes through another word, or a half-drawn glyph reads as a different letter. **Fix:** reveal names by whole word or with a mask; list the reveal's prefixes and check those frames (`references/copy-and-type.md` §8).

## D. Colour and surface
- **D1 Generated gradients.** Indigo or violet → blue, a rainbow or aurora wash behind everything, mesh blobs with no job, banded CSS gradients (HB7). **Fix:** grounds are neutral; a gradient is a device that does a job (a floor glow, a dithered colour field that the cut flies through, type built from a moving plate), baked and dithered (`references/lighting.md`, `references/brand-and-color.md`).
- **D2 Neon on black.** An accent in the neon region (L ≥ 0.85 with C ≥ 0.15, L ≥ 0.80 with C ≥ 0.18, or C ≥ 0.27) on an ink stage (HB6). **Fix:** lower the lightness or chroma; the accent is a signal, not a light source.
- **D3 The beige default.** A beige or cream ground with an orange or terracotta accent (HB5). **Fix:** neutral or cool papers (around `#F7F8F8`) and an accent from an allowed family; warm only when the brand supplied it.
- **D4 Glass and glow as style.** Frosted UI panels everywhere, an outer glow on text, a halo round every element (HB6, HB8). **Fix:** shadows from one key in the ground's hue (`references/lighting.md`); on ink, a luminance step plus a 1 px top hairline at 8–12% white; glow and glass only as devices.
- **D5 An accent without a meaning.** Used everywhere, used for two opposite meanings, or two near-identical reds in the code. **Fix:** one token, one meaning, at its share of the picture over neutral grounds, one flood at most; measure it with `look-audit.py` (`accent-share`) or `palette.py --cover`.
- **D6 Filler decoration.** Particles, bokeh, starfields, code or ASCII rain (HB10), ghost text at 3–8%, grain that does nothing or changes per scene, and the quieter kind: faint rectangles floating round a line, blank cards at depth, an empty pill behind a sentence, corner brackets, gobo shadows on the ground. **Fix:** every element in frame has a job (reveal, route, validate, emphasise, carry the product's real state) or is cut; a plate at depth carries a real screen, a mark or a line (`fx/DepthStack` `carries`); grain is none, or global 1.5–2% keyed to the output frame. `look-audit.py` fails faint shapes that carry no text, icon or mark (`filler-shape`); `lint-film.mjs` warns on blank boxes generated at depth (`filler-plate`).
- **D7 Muddy washes.** A white radial wash turns ink cards into grey buttons; a lit panel's face lifted into grey; a dull grey stage with a colour cast. **Fix:** ink stays ink: no veil over the product, a face lifts 5 % toward the key at most (`fx/Lit`), and the light is the rim and the shadow.
- **D8 Pure black holes.** Full-frame `#000` stages that crush under encoding. **Fix:** ink at OKLCH L 0.15–0.18, tinted 0.003–0.008 chroma toward the accent.
- **D9 House-style convergence.** The starter's or Tessel's palette or mark, the starter's face kept without choosing its look, a block-and-accent-dot mark, or a light paper stage kept because the starter had one. **Fix:** three adjectives → family, stage, accent and shapes, then a sheet of 6–10 mark directions (`references/brand-and-color.md` §2, §4).
- **D10 A default palette.** Framework blue (the `#3B82F6` / `#2563EB` / `#1D4ED8` family, hue about 260) on flat greys (`#808080`-style, chroma 0), pure black and white: read as "template" before any motion plays. **Fix:** derive the stage, neutrals and accent from the world of the name and the product, tint the neutrals (chroma 0.003–0.010), move the accent's hue and chroma off the framework values (`references/brand-and-color.md` §9).
- **D11 A colourless film.** Restraint turned into no colour: a mid-film frame at thumbnail size shows no brand colour at all, and the accent never carries the product's action. **Fix:** the accent visibly marks the key beats (the product acting, the payoff) and the stage has a tone from the brand's world; check a mid-film frame at 480 px; on a key beat `python3 scripts/palette.py --cover <still> --accent '#…'` should not read near 0%.
- **D12 A mark that misreads.** At 64 px in half a second it reads as a common symbol: a minus or plus, an emoticon, a menu or "more" icon, a padlock, a play button, ±. **Fix:** the misread test on the mark sheet; change the geometry (an asymmetric break usually fixes a symmetric symbol) and test again (`references/brand-and-color.md` §4).
- **D13 Haze behind the hero.** Soft colour pools, a light pool or a radial wash behind a product window or a line of type, a slight white haze over a dark ground, a vignette that reads as fog: it reads as a stock gradient backdrop on a light stage and as glow on a dark one. **Fix:** light is motivated and subtle: a flat stage, or one key falloff from one side of 0.02–0.10 L (`fx/Grounds` flat or wash), with a pool only where the place has a lamp or a window and the film names it; when colour must move, a crisp-edged band or a full-bleed accent ground on the beat. `look-audit.py` fails the ground's light rising to a pool inside the frame (`ground-haze`).
- **D15 A monochrome accent wash.** Grounds, surfaces, shadows and graded pictures all at the accent's hue, so the whole film is one colour: saturated pixels on most frames, almost no neutral. **Fix:** neutral grounds (C ≤ 0.025), the accent at its share, the pictures in their own colour (`assets.mjs grade` natural), an occasional second brand hue for UI states or imagery (`references/brand-and-color.md`).
- **D16 A world with nothing of the product in it.** Generic cards and shapes on a flat ground that could belong to any product. **Fix:** build the product's world: its UI at film size in code, and real photos or footage of the people who use it, the places and the things it touches on the beats that show them (`references/imagery.md` §0), chosen with taste and finished like a studio would (`fx/Pictures`).
- **D17 Grey placeholder cards.** Empty slabs or glyphless grey bars standing in for UI, a line that says "Placeholder", "Label text" or lorem. **Fix:** real product parts with real strings and numbers (`src/kit`); `lint-film` (`placeholder-copy`, `bar-text`), the layout audit's placeholder floor and `copy-check.py --ui src` fail them.
- **D18 A drawing where a photo belongs.** A person drawn as an egg-shaped head or a silhouette, a landscape of flat bands, a product as two rectangles, a wall or a stream of coloured squares where portraits or creations should be, a flat rectangle where a picture should lean in space. It reads as a placeholder the moment it appears. **Fix:** a real photo or clip (`scripts/assets.mjs search ... --people`, Burst, ISO Republic, Coverr, Mixkit, or the user's own), cropped on its subject, on a rounded card with a rim and a shadow, leaning where the shot is in space, sharp where the eye goes and soft away from it, smeared along its travel, graded into the film (`fx/Pictures`: PhotoCard, ArcRiver, PhotoWall, InlinePhoto, FootageWindow; `references/imagery.md` §4). `lint-film` and the layout audit fail the kit's drawn figures above a small UI avatar (`drawn-figure`). Drawn illustration is for the mark or a mascot only, and optional (a wordmark or a simple geometric mark is often better): never drawn objects or scenes; light and effects are fine.
- **D19 Rough detail.** Close, but not looked at: cards on a straight row at even spacing where the shot calls for a curve with depth blur, a flat ground where the colour should gather along an edge, an upright card where it should lean back for its close-up, an edge with no rim, a dashed line that slides instead of drawing on, a fade where a capsule should widen from its centre, a system arrow where the cursor should have a finish. **Fix:** the detail pass (`scripts/detail-pair.py`, `assets/critics/detail.md`, `references/review-loop.md` §2) and its primitives (`fx/Pictures` ArcRiver, `fx/Washes` tilted, `fx/Cards` CARD_TILT and `reveal`, `fx/Rim` KeyRim, `fx/DrawPath`, `fx/Cursor` plane).
- **D14 The stock tech palette.** Cool grey paper, navy ink and a teal or framework-blue accent: correct, and anonymous. **Fix:** tint the stage from the brand's world (pine, slate, ink-navy, a deep red, a clean yellow) and let the accent carry one meaning (`references/brand-and-color.md` §8–10).

## E. Layout and framing
- **E1 The SaaS layout.** Text left, UI card right on white; a line flush left at the top or the bottom; a line on top with a big picture below it. **Fix:** the line centred in the middle of the frame on its own clean field, the product before or after it (or stepping back behind it, `fx/ReadBack`), full-bleed when it is the shot; never a lower-third subtitle by default; one focal action per shot.
- **E2 Voids and edge bands.** 20% of the frame empty under the product, a dark window edge 20–130 px wide at the border, a UI header over 120 px of nothing. **Fix:** clamp the camera so the product covers the frame; overscan moving layers 5–8%; collapse a section until its content arrives.
- **E3 No margins.** Less than 96 px at the sides or 64 px top and bottom, or less than 24 px of headroom at maximum punch. **Fix:** check the safe area on the punch frame with `scripts/layout-audit.sh`.
- **E4 Crops that slice glyphs.** "aft", ":00", or text cut by a card edge moving across it. **Fix:** crop on column gutters; reveal text only after the edge has passed.
- **E5 Cropped social versions.** A 9:16 or 1:1 cut out of the 16:9 master. **Fix:** re-lay separate compositions from the same timeline (`references/formats.md`).
- **E6 A lockup lost in a void.** The resolved lockup spans under a quarter of the frame's width and can't be read at 480 px. **Fix:** 28–45% of the width at its resolved size (`references/brand-and-color.md` §6).
- **E7 A small card in empty space.** The product shown as a floating card whose type is unreadable on a phone. **Fix:** the object at 50-80 % of the frame width, or push in until the working part fills the frame (`references/product-ui.md` §1).
- **E8 An empty vertical frame.** A 9:16 frame laid out like a wide one: a strip of content in the middle, the bottom 40% bare, 16:9 type sizes. **Fix:** the content's mass in the middle of the safe band (y 270–1500 at 1080 × 1920), the product filling or bleeding past the band, type 1.3–1.5× the 16:9 sizes; the mass check and layout sketch are in `references/formats.md` §7.
- **E9 The screen recording.** A page or app filling the frame edge to edge for seconds, the camera crawling or jumping across it: a zoomed screen capture, every frame alike. **Fix:** pull back so the page is an object on the stage, isolate the card or clause that matters, a new picture every 1-2 s; a full-frame macro is a 1-2 s accent (`references/product-ui.md` §1).

## F. Motion and easing
- **F1 The default entrance.** Everything fades in, or enters with `y: 30, opacity: 0`. **Fix:** one reveal system per role, with physical entrances: a mask sweep, a birth from a gap, a morph.
- **F2 Uniform timing.** Every move 0.4–0.5 s on one out-curve. **Fix:** duration = 0.35 s + 1.35 ms per px (camera clamped to 0.6–2.4 s); the slowest move ≥ 3× the fastest; about 3 easing characters per film.
- **F3 Bounce everywhere.** Back-out, elastic, overshoot on every card, a "playful" pop on a badge (HB11). **Fix:** critical damping or an ease by default; 1–7% overshoot only on a true landing; ≤ 2 landing springs per film.
- **F4 Easing the wrong way.** Ease-in entrances, ease-out exits, expo-out fade-outs (a card at 55% in one frame), a statement that fades out on its own. **Fix:** entrances on arrive curves, exits on leave curves at speed, cut on their fastest frame; a short fade only inside a moving exit (`fade-exit`, `ease-family`).
- **F5 Half-sine punches.** A pulse on a hard window: velocity clunks at both ends and a peak 6–8 f after its sound. **Fix:** `hitPulse(t, 2, 5)` for every punch and tick.
- **F6 Linear zooms and walls.** Scale lerped linearly; a camera that stops dead (1.46 → 0.17 px/f in one frame). **Fix:** `lmix` for scale; `E.rest` for moves that end at rest.
- **F7 Stall, then lurch.** Two moves ease to rest, then the next starts at speed. **Fix:** overlap consecutive moves by ~20 f, or start the second on a zero-slope curve.
- **F8 Everything moving at once.** Idle bobbing, pulsing badges, an ambient zoom on every scene. **Fix:** one hero motion plus ≤ 2 supporting ones; stillness after motion is a tool.
- **F9 Batch landings.** 6–7 objects hitting on the same frame. **Fix:** stagger 0–3 f within the group, with the first object on the beat.
- **F10 Shape snaps.** The aspect ratio flips in the last 1–2 frames because one ease drove size and position. **Fix:** shape settles 6 f before position; land with a contact squash (1.08 / 0.9 → 1 over 8 f).
- **F11 Springs on type.** Words or letters arriving on an overshooting spring, a bounce or a slam on every line, letters rising one by one on copy. **Fix:** the canonical word reveal (`snapOut`, `Words`, `Chain`); emphasis from the transient accent, a hue sweep or an oversized landing; per-letter motion only inside a line's own text animation (`TypeLine`) or the one word that earns it (`lint-film.mjs` `text-spring`, `per-letter`).
- **F12 Fade-in words.** Words that fade up from nothing on a symmetric curve. **Fix:** a third of the word visible on its first frame, 90 % by about 0.25 s (`word-first-frame`, `arrival-front-load`).
- **F13 A metronome stagger.** Every gap identical. **Fix:** the reading cadence (gaps by word length), irregular UI staggers (variation ≥ 0.15), accelerating runs into a payoff (`stagger-shape`).
- **F14 Aimless motion.** A camera that floats and pans back and forth for no reason, cards bobbing or breathing while they are held, panels tilted diagonally at rest. **Fix:** the camera is still unless a shot declares a move that starts, travels and lands; held UI holds still; cards face the camera (`camera-wander`, `camera-float`, `idle-bob`, `card-tilt`). A hold may be still; only 2.5 s where nothing changes is dead (`dead-stretch`).
- **F15 Bounce as polish.** Overshoot on cards, chips and menus that merely arrive. **Fix:** one spring class per film, everything else critically damped (`spring-budget`).

## G. Transitions
- **G1 Crossfade by default.** Or two misregistered outlines double-exposed for 26 f. **Fix:** morph the container or clip it with an inset; only one outline ever exists (`references/transitions.md`).
- **G2 Presets and repeats.** Glitch, RGB split as decoration, light leak, film burn, swirl, ripple, dreamy zoom, a fade through white; the same iris three times. **Fix:** transitions built from what is on screen, any kind at most 3 times except the signature (`references/transitions.md` §1).
- **G3 A pop where a move was meant.** A lockup gone in 2 f, a pill that changes state in 1 f. **Fix:** exit by reversing the entrance, or cut deliberately on the beat.
- **G4 Velocity mismatch at a cut.** **Fix:** the outgoing move launches into the cut (its last frame the fastest), the incoming shot opens at 0.4-1.4× that speed and decays ×0.78-0.93 a frame (`launch`, `carry`; `carried-cut`).
- **G6 Dissolves between shots.** A soft cross of two unrelated pictures, or a fade to black mid-film. **Fix:** a carried seam; dissolves only inside UI, at most 8 f; a fade only ends the film (`dissolve-detect`, `seam-plan`).
- **G5 Seam stutter and dips.** A duplicated frame at a seam (stop, hold, go), or a crossfade through black that dips ~25% in luminance. **Fix:** show the rest pose once; carry luminance flips on a moving shape.

## H. Camera
- **H1 Floating screenshots.** Tilted screenshot cards, fly-throughs of screenshot fields, a device mockup orbiting in a void. **Fix:** real 3D on real components, perspective 1800–2600 px, one camera axis per act; hardware only when hardware is the product.
- **H2 Locked-off shots.** Nothing moving for more than 0.8 s. **Fix:** a creep of 2-15 %/s or a drift of 6-90 px/s, with parallax between planes (`references/camera.md` §7).
- **H3 Invisible drift.** 1 → 1.05 over 2 s on a logo reads as dead. **Fix:** keep the still part of a logo hold short (36–80 f after a 60–80 f assembly), or push 10–20% over a longer one; UI holds drift 0.5–1.5 px/f. A reading hold under text is different: there a 0.3–1 px/f drift or a 1–3% creep is the right amount of life, because the eye is busy reading. The drift that reads as dead is on a logo or a frame where nothing is being read.
- **H4 Strobing fast moves.** A camera-real move without blur under the camera policy. **Fix:** declare the blur policy (`LOOK.blur`), and a `CAMERA_BLUR` range for a fast camera moment inside a crisp film (`references/finishing.md` §2).
- **H5 Moves too fast to blur.** An element crossing 150–1,000 px in a frame: blur turns it into a long smear with stepped copies. **Fix:** keep any one element under about 60–80 px/f; faster than that, redesign the move (a cut on the beat, a match cut, a mask wipe, a shorter distance). `measure-speed.py` names the frames.

## I. UI depiction
- **I1 Generic or pictured UI.** Fake KPIs, up-and-right charts, random numbers, stock icons (HB9), a screenshot zoomed until soft. **Fix:** the product's own surfaces as real components with named data from one module (`references/product-ui.md`).
- **I2 Data that disagrees.** A date on the wrong weekday, stale labels after a move, a counter saying 7 over 12 items, the product replanning the past. **Fix:** derive every label from `data.ts` and assert it.
- **I3 State that doesn't count.** A counter frozen during the action, then flipped in one frame, or a counter or check that changes a beat after the thing that caused it landed. **Fix:** change on each visible event's landing frame, computed on whole frames (`fd`), from the same exported frames as its sound (`references/product-ui.md` §4.2).
- **I4 Z-order bugs.** A badge bitten by the next avatar, a lifted card drawn under a grounded one, a toast over the result. **Fix:** paint sorted by z; badges get a 2 px ring in the background colour.
- **I5 Input no real app has.** A caret floating 8–75 px from the text, a chip whose weight animates and reflows the line. **Fix:** padding grows with the reveal; weight stays constant; animate only fill and colour.
- **I6 A robotic cursor.** Straight paths at constant speed, a parked cursor, or any cursor on a product that acts alone. **Fix:** arc 15–25% of the path, travel 36–48 f, press to 0.88 over 4 f, ≤ 1 click per 30 f; no cursor once the product is autonomous.
- **I7 Story and data disagree.** The person who paid is shown owing, a ratio doesn't match the numbers beside it, one figure appears twice in a frame. **Fix:** assert the story's facts in `data.ts` (`references/product-ui.md` §2).
- **I8 Text covered by a moving element.** A chip, card or the device slides over a label the viewer is reading (a badge flying across a list's labels). **Fix:** route paths through gutters, or paint the text above the mover; assert the path with `covers()` and tag movers `data-mover` so `layout-audit.sh` fails it (`references/product-ui.md` §4.5).

## J. Sound
- **J1 Library sound.** A stock whoosh on every cut, meme effects, riser → crash on everything, the same whoosh and pop in every film. **Fix:** the earshot kit cast for this film's actions (`kit.py cast`: each action its own sound, the last film's signature sounds avoided; a free no-credit real effect where the bank is weak), levelled over the track; about 2 whooshes a film, only on camera-scale moves and logo zooms, onset 100–450 ms ahead, apex on the visual peak, travelling with the motion; risers end in a stop, a filter drop or a short gap (`references/sound.md` §12).
- **J2 Out of lock.** Audio leading picture by more than 45 ms, impacts on the settle instead of the contact, thuds for off-frame landings, a sounded class of action with some instances silent (5 of 14 presses). **Fix:** cues exported from the picture code; impacts on the contact frame; ±1 f; every instance of a sounded class at one level.
- **J3 Masked effects.** Effects 13–25 dB under the music in their own band. **Fix:** each effect at its `bed_db` over the bed as it plays; a stop-down before a hit, a drop or a swell; never a duck.
- **J4 Out of key.** Untuned booms, snaps and ticks; a riser landing a tritone away. **Fix:** tune the kit's tonal roles to the track's key (`kit.py tune`); at most two pitched effects in any 4 s.
- **J5 The wrong ending.** Clicks at buffer ends, an ending cut mid-note or at full level, or a logo slam as the loudest moment. **Fix:** 6 ms cos² tails, last 480 samples at zero; the drop on the hero reveal is the loudest moment (limiter gain reduction < 1.5 dB); the ending subtracts: the music stops or filters down as the logo forms, 0.2–0.7 s of near-silence, one soft resolved element on the wordmark, a 0.5–2.5 s tail to below −60 dBFS.
- **J6 Typing rattle.** Bright clicks on every typed character fusing into a buzz, a random timbre per key, typing laid over the full groove. **Fix:** `"typing": "auto"`: a dark modal tock per key (fixed per key, ±1.2% per press) up to 25 chars/s, one blip per word above; word starts accented, fractional key frames; `score.py` thins to about 12 keys/s (`references/sound.md` §5.5c).
- **J7 Sound on every picture event.** A whoosh or hit on every cut, scrolls, progress bars and rolling counters sounded, an even effects layer through the film; or the opposite, a cut or whip peaking a quarter-second after the beat. **Fix:** cuts and velocity peaks on the grid, carried by the music; section changes and the hero reveal on the music's own event (drop, stop, re-entry); about 5 designed effects per 10 s on discrete state changes, clustered where the music is sparse. `av-audit.py` suggests a music event when one of the 3 biggest picture changes has no audio onset (`references/timing-grid.md`).
- **J8 A flat mix.** Loudness range under 5 LU on a film of 20 s or more; the payoff barely louder than the rest. **Fix:** thin out before the turn, true silence before the drop, the payoff 2–3 LU above the median momentary loudness (`references/sound.md`).
- **J9 A soft hook.** The soundtrack fades in over the first bar (a pad or drone swelling from about −25 dB), so the first second sounds like nothing is happening yet. **Fix:** a sound with a visible cause on frame 0; the intro may be soft (a drone or a 150–450 ms entry, 5–12 LU under the body for 2–3 s) but enters with intent; `score.py` warns when the first 0.5 s sits more than 12 dB under the median momentary loudness (`references/sound.md` §5.3).
- **J10 The wrong energy for the brand.** Drops onto silence, sub booms and risers under a calm brand (trailer grammar), or a timid bed under a fast launch. **Fix:** pick the personality row first (`references/sound.md` §5.5a); calm brands get soft mallets and one gentle chord.

- **J11 Ducking.** The music dipping under every effect or every typed key. **Fix:** effects levelled over the bed; a stop-down only before a hit, a drop or a swell, at most 3 per 10 s (`references/sound.md` §8.1).
- **J12 A generated bed, or no music.** A synth pad, a drone or a band made in code, a melody of tuned effects, or a film with effects only. **Fix:** a licensed, human-made, instrumental track on every film (`references/music.md`).

## K. Pacing
- **K1 Weak hook.** 1–2 s of near-blank frame (mean difference < 0.05), which a muted feed reads as a stalled player. **Fix:** frame 0 already composed and moving; a visible change about every 1 s (0.5 s in crisp), with slow life between.
- **K2 Dead holds.** A frozen stretch: more than 72 f (48 f in crisp) with no visible life, such as a logo held 1.6–2.4 s at a frame difference of 0.03–0.08. A reading hold that drifts 0.3–1 px/f or creeps 1–3% is alive, and is not a dead hold. **Fix:** every hold builds or drifts, with no tick planted inside a read; a designed freeze is ≤ 15 f and sits exactly on a silence.
- **K3 Cramming.** 17 scenes in 32 s, or 3 features in 5 s. **Fix:** 6–8 story beats per 30 s (8–10 in crisp) and 1–2 capabilities.
- **K4 Flat energy.** A middle 10× calmer than the rest, peaks with no calm between them, or no peak at all. **Fix:** an energy arc: an early peak, a designed lull, the biggest peak at 75–90%; one hero move per bar in calm, one motion peak per bar with 30–70 f of calm between in crisp. Reading holds with slow drift are the cruise between peaks, not flat energy.
- **K5 Off-music timing.** Cuts and hits that ignore the music, or every cut on a downbeat. **Fix:** structure on the track's events, graphic cuts on its accents, varied shot lengths (`references/timing-grid.md` §4b).
- **K6 A soft first second.** Frame 0 is composed, but only the opening line moves and the problem isn't felt by 1 s; paired with a fade-in (J9), a muted feed scrolls past. **Fix:** the device and the product's state both move from frame 0, something visibly changes about every 1 s (0.5 s in crisp), the problem is felt by 1 s.
- **K7 Hurry.** Word entries under 24 f, staggers under 8 f, every hold at its minimum, fragments arriving back to back with no picture between, text landing during the hero move, or fast moves while words are read. **Fix:** `PACE.calm`; text and the hero action take turns (a picture beat of 26–90 f between fragments, nothing fast during a read); a fast move is rare and lands in a text-free stretch (`references/timing-grid.md` §9).

- **K8 A sampler.** A new device every half second, fourteen tricks in fourteen seconds. **Fix:** one device per beat (about 1.5-2.5 s), one signature shot, devices from many families (`references/devices/INDEX.md`).
- **K9 One device everywhere.** The same small device (four corner squares, a pill, a sweep) on every beat. **Fix:** a device is the film's identity only when the direction says so; otherwise each beat draws its own.

## L. Finishing
- **L1 Pops and ghost frames.** Single-frame pops, or stale frames that land on different frames each render (`will-change` on a layer with an animated blur). **Fix:** never promote a layer with blur animating inside it; render twice and compare hashes.
- **L2 Stepped blur ramps.** An animated CSS blur that jumps sharp → soft in one frame (Laplacian variance 1416 → 83). **Fix:** `src/fx/RackFocus.tsx`, a cross-fade of a sharp copy and a constant-blur copy.
- **L3 Pixel-snap stairs.** Slow moves stepping in whole pixels every 6–8 f (a fractional left/top combined with a transform). **Fix:** all translation inside one transform.
- **L4 Hairline crawl.** Strokes under 1.5 px or gaps under 8 units crawling as dashes under scale or perspective. **Fix:** thicker strokes, wider gaps, hairlines faded out at small scale (`references/chromium-rendering.md`).
- **L5 Banding.** A CSS gradient spanning fewer than ~30 code values shows rings. **Fix:** dithered PNG stages from `scripts/dither-gradient.py`.
- **L6 Bad motion blur.** Stamped copies of text, greys tinted cyan, blur smeared across a cut, double carets. **Fix:** float accumulation with samples ≤ 3 px apart, clamped to the act, discrete state from whole frames.
- **L7 Sloppy delivery.** An untagged BT.601 preview, JPEG q80 intermediates, CRF 18, Remotion's AAC priming (~43 ms late). **Fix:** BT.709 tags everywhere, JPEG q95, CRF 14–16, a muted render muxed by ffmpeg, then `scripts/check-sync.py`.
- **L8 A cluttered delivery folder.** Contact sheets, test crops and reports next to the film. **Fix:** deliverables at the top level, QA material in `qa/` (`scripts/deliver.sh` writes it that way).
